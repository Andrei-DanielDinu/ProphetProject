# The Lore: Temporal Assumptions

> "Time is not a straight highway of inevitable progress. It is an ocean where currents, storms, and reefs can sink any civilization that forgets to maintain its foundations."

---

## 1. Timeline Conventions

- **Origin Anchor**: Year 2026 CE.
- **Destination Anchor**: Approximately 2975 BCE (Early Dynastic Egypt, First Dynasty).
- **Elapsed Planning Clock**:
  - `Year 0`: Physical arrival of the Prophet.
  - `Year 5`: Completion of Phase 0 (first workshop-school operating).
  - `Year 100`: Completion of Phase 1 (stabilized food reserves, standardized metrology).
  - `Year 500`: Completion of Phase 2 (regional trade networks, bronze metallurgy, law).
  - `Year 1200`: Completion of Phase 3 (waterwheels, bloomery iron, printing press, optics).
  - `Year 2200`: Completion of Phase 4 (steam power, machine tools, industrial chemistry).
  - `Year 3200`: Completion of Phase 5 (AC electricity, transcontinental radio, Haber-Bosch).
  - `Year 4500`: Completion of Phase 6 (semiconductors, nuclear power, rocketry, CERN, ISS, LLMs).
  - `Year 5000`: Phase 7 (Consolidation and Threat Mitigation).

---

## 2. Non-Linearity and Scenario Branching

The roadmap avoids the illusion of strict linear chronology:
- **Uneven Diffusion**: Certain regions will develop waterwheels or blast furnaces centuries before others due to coal, ore, and river access.
- **Technological Reversals**: Natural disasters, political collapses, or pandemics can cause temporary de-industrialization. Redundant distributed archives and decentralized workshop networks ensure capabilities can be recovered quickly.
- **Configurable Baselines**: While Early Dynastic Egypt serves as the primary scenario, the dependency model is designed to support alternative starting points (e.g., Mesopotamia, Indus Valley, Yellow River, or post-collapse scenarios).
