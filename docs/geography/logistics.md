# Geography: Transportation Energy and Logistics Economics

> "Transport cost dictates what can be made. If shipping an ore 100 miles costs more food than the ore is worth in metal, the mine will be abandoned."

---

## 1. Relative Energy Costs Across Transport Modes

Moving a 1-metric-ton load across 1 kilometer requires vastly different caloric inputs depending on the mechanical medium:

| Mode | Energy Cost (kJ / ton-km) | Relative Cost Index | Limiting Constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Human Porterage** | 3,500 | 100.0 | Maximum payload 25 kg per person; consumes load over long distances |
| **Pack Donkey** | 1,200 | 34.0 | Maximum payload 80–100 kg; requires fodder and water caches |
| **Ox Cart on Dirt Road** | 800 | 23.0 | Slow speed (3 km/h); axle friction; road mud in rains |
| **Nile Fluvial Barge** | 80 | 2.3 | River navigation limits; seasonal water levels |
| **Maritime Sailing Ship** | 40 | 1.1 | Storms; seasonal winds; port harbor availability |
| **Steam Railway (Phase 4)** | 30 | 0.8 | Capital cost of iron rail beds and bridges |
| **Bulk Maritime Supertanker (Phase 5+)** | 10 | 0.3 | Draft depth; canal dimensions (Suez/Panama) |

### The Water Transit Imperative
Early industrialization must cluster along navigable waterways. Overland bulk transit of iron ore, limestone, and coal is uneconomical prior to the railway; waterborne transit provides the necessary thermodynamic leverage.
