# Baseline: The Ancient Near East and Trade Networks

> "Egypt was never an isolated island. It was part of a dynamic maritime and terrestrial corridor connecting the Levant, Sinai, Nubia, Anatolia, and Mesopotamia."

---

## 1. Regional Neighbors and Resource Endowments

Around 3000–2900 BCE, the broader Near East featured established urban centers and specialized trade:

1. **The Levant and Byblos**: Major source of tall structural timber (cedar, fir, cypress) essential for large sea-going hulls and roof beams, traded for Egyptian gold and grain.
2. **Sinai Peninsula (Wadi Maghara, Serabit el-Khadim)**: Rich deposits of turquoise and copper malachite ores worked by seasonal expeditions.
3. **Nubia (Lower Nile south of First Cataract)**: Vital gateway for gold, ebony, ivory, cattle, and incense routes leading to sub-Saharan Africa.
4. **Anatolia and Central Asia**: Early metallurgical experimentation; essential overland links to cassiterite tin deposits required for bronze.
5. **Mesopotamia (Early Dynastic Sumer)**: Urban city-states with advanced cuneiform administrative accounting, wheel transport, and brick vaulted construction.
