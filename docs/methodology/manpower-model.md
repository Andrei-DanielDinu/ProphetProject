# Methodology: Manpower and Caloric Balances

> "Headcount is not a workforce plan. A thousand hungry workers without tools, shelter, or clean water build nothing but epidemics."

---

## 1. The Labor Accounting Framework

Work is modeled in **person-days** broken down by:
- **Skill Domain**: Unskilled manual labor, apprentice, master artisan, surveyor/scribe, research engineer.
- **Seasonality**: In agrarian baseline societies, manual labor is bound to agricultural rhythms (e.g., Egyptian *akhet* flood season vs *shemu* harvest season).
- **Physical Output Limits**: A healthy manual laborer can deliver approximately $0.1\text{ horsepower-hours}$ ($\approx 75\text{ Watts}$) of continuous mechanical work over an 8-hour shift ($\approx 2.16\text{ MJ/day}$).

---

## 2. Food Surplus as the Enabler of Specialization

Non-farming artisans, scribes, and metallurgists produce zero calories. They can exist only if the farming population produces an agricultural surplus:

$$\text{Surplus Ratio } S = \frac{\text{Total Grain Harvest} - \text{Farmer Consumption} - \text{Seed Reserve}}{\text{Farmer Consumption}}$$

- At $S = 0.10$ (Early Dynastic baseline), 10 farming households support only 1 non-farming specialist household.
- At $S = 0.35$ (Iron plows and waterwheels), 3 farming households support 1 specialist household.
- At $S = 3.50$ (Haber-Bosch and steam mechanization), 1 farming household supports 3.5 specialist households.
- At $S = 50.0$ (Modern combine harvesters and robotic agriculture), $2\%$ of the population feeds the remaining $98\%$.
