# Methodology: Resource and Process Accounting

> "A mine is not a store shelf. Material must be discovered, extracted from host rock, concentrated, transported across geography, refined, tested, and recycled."

---

## 1. The Full Material Processing Chain

For every physical input, the project tracks the entire cradle-to-gate lifecycle:

$$\text{Deposit} \to \text{Extraction} \to \text{Beneficiation} \to \text{Transport} \to \text{Processing} \to \text{Testing} \to \text{Use} \to \text{Recycling}$$

### The Six Mineral Tiers
1. **Occurrence**: The mineral exists somewhere within the Earth's crust.
2. **Resource**: The deposit is concentrated enough to be identifiable.
3. **Technically Recoverable Resource**: Extraction is physically possible with available tools (e.g., bronze chisels and fire-setting vs deep diesel drilling).
4. **Accessible Reserve**: Located within reachable trade or transport boundaries.
5. **Processed Material**: Ingot or chemical reagent delivered to the workshop door.
6. **Recycled Stock**: Secondary metal reclaimed from worn-out or scrap tools.

---

## 2. Mass and Energy Balances

All chemical and metallurgical processes must balance mass ($m_{\text{in}} = m_{\text{out}}$) and energy ($E_{\text{in}} = E_{\text{out}}$):

$$\sum m_{\text{reactants}} = \sum m_{\text{products}} + \sum m_{\text{slag}} + \sum m_{\text{emissions}}$$

No process may assume $100\%$ recovery efficiency. Slag losses, oxidation burning, volatilization, and machining swarf must be quantified explicitly.
