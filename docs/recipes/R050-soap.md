# R050 — Soap (Saponification: Natron Lye + Tallow)

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R050 · HARD SOAP BAR                                     ║
║  End product: 8 kg hard soap bars, castile-equivalent quality   ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHAT YOU GET                                                    ║
║    Hard white/cream soap bars. pH: 9–10. Lathers in water.      ║
║    Removes grease, dirt, bacteria from skin and surfaces.        ║
║                                                                  ║
║    WHY THIS IS PRIORITY #1 IN YEAR 1:                           ║
║    — Hand-washing with soap reduces dysentery and typhoid by    ║
║      60–80% (Nile water is contaminated with fecal pathogens).  ║
║    — Visually spectacular to Egyptians (priests value cleanliness║
║    — Gives Prophet credibility as a "purifier" before Pharaoh. ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  CHEMISTRY (know this — it cannot be guessed)                   ║
║    NaOH + fat → soap + glycerol                                  ║
║    Specifically: 3 NaOH + (C17H35COO)₃C₃H₅ →                  ║
║                  3 C17H35COONa + C₃H₅(OH)₃                     ║
║    (3 sodium hydroxide + tristearin → 3 sodium stearate + glycerol)║
║                                                                  ║
║    You CANNOT use natron directly — Na₂CO₃ is too weak to fully ║
║    saponify fat at ambient temperature. Must make NaOH first.   ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS (per 8 kg hard soap batch)                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) 7 kg rendered animal tallow           [→ R051]              ║
║     Cattle/sheep suet. Must be RENDERED (purified):             ║
║     free of water, blood, meat tissue.                           ║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) 1 kg sodium hydroxide (NaOH solution)  [→ R052]             ║
║     Concentration: 30–35% NaOH by weight (lye solution).       ║
║     Made by causticising natron with quicklime.                  ║
║                                                                  ║
║  C) 500g salt (NaCl, for salting-out)      [→ R000-L] ■ TERM.  ║
║     Saturated brine added at end. Salt forces soap to precipitate║
║     (rise to surface) while glycerol stays in water below.      ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • Large clay pot, 15 L capacity            [→ R020]          ║
║    • Wooden paddle for stirring               [→ R000-I] TERM.  ║
║    • Fire and pot support stones              [→ R010]          ║
║    • Soap mold: flat wooden tray, 30×20×5 cm [→ R000-I] TERM.  ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  CONDITIONS                                                      ║
║    • Tallow temperature at mixing: 60–70°C (liquid but not       ║
║      boiling — bubbling makes froth that slows reaction).        ║
║    • Lye temperature at mixing: 30–40°C (let it cool after      ║
║      making before adding to fat).                               ║
║    • Reaction: exothermic (releases heat). Keep mixing.          ║
║    • Salting-out water: near-boiling (90°C) with 300g salt       ║
║      dissolved per litre.                                        ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — PREPARE TALLOW (previous day)                          ║
║    → See R051 for full tallow rendering recipe.                  ║
║    Result needed here: 7 kg clear, clean, solid tallow.         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — PREPARE NaOH LYE SOLUTION (R052)                      ║
║    → Full recipe in R052. Requires 2 kg natron + 1 kg CaO.     ║
║    Result: ~3 L of 30% NaOH solution. Use 1 kg (1 L) for this. ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — MELT TALLOW                                            ║
║    3a. Place 7 kg tallow in large clay pot.                     ║
║    3b. Heat over low fire to 65°C (barely smoking — oil just     ║
║        starts to thin and flow. If smoking heavily: too hot).   ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 4 — SAPONIFY                                               ║
║    4a. Remove pot from direct flame (keep near embers).          ║
║    4b. Add NaOH lye (1 kg, cooled to 35°C) SLOWLY to fat.       ║
║        Pour in thin stream while stirring vigorously.            ║
║    4c. Stir constantly for 30–60 minutes.                        ║
║        Visual guide:                                             ║
║        — Start: oily, watery mix (separates if stopped).        ║
║        — After 15 min: thickens slightly, like cream.           ║
║        — After 30 min: "trace" appears (drip off paddle, leaves  ║
║          trail on surface that holds 3–5 seconds). ← TRACE. Done║
║        — TRACE = saponification complete. Stop heating.          ║
║    4d. If no trace after 60 min: lye too dilute or fat too cold. ║
║        Warm gently to 65°C, stir 20 more minutes.               ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 5 — SALT OUT (optional for hard bar soap)                  ║
║    5a. Add 2 L of hot saturated brine (90°C, 300g NaCl/L) to   ║
║        the soap pot. Stir briefly.                               ║
║    5b. The soap mass separates: curds rise to top, grey liquid   ║
║        (glycerol + excess lye + water) sinks below.             ║
║    5c. Scoop soap curds into wooden mold tray. Pack firmly.     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 6 — SET AND CUT                                            ║
║    6a. Allow soap to set in mold 24–48 hours in shade.           ║
║    6b. Cut into bars: 10×7×3 cm (convenient grip size).          ║
║    6c. Cure bars: stack with air gaps, dry 4 weeks. During       ║
║        curing: remaining NaOH neutralises into soap salts →     ║
║        reduces pH from 13 to 9–10 (safe for skin).              ║
║    6d. TEST: lick one corner of bar. If strong burning sensation:║
║        excess lye — cure 2 more weeks. If mild/neutral: ready.  ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  YIELD: 7 kg tallow + 1 kg NaOH → ~8 kg hard soap.             ║
║  CYCLE TIME: 2 hours making + 48h setting + 4 weeks curing.    ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PRODUCTION SCALING                                              ║
║    • 1 batch/day × 8 kg = 56 kg soap/week.                      ║
║    • 1,000-worker operation needs ~20 kg soap/week (20g/person/ ║
║      day for 2 hand-washes). 1 batch/week is sufficient.        ║
║    • DISTRIBUTION: each team leader gets 500g/week to distribute.║
║      Soap distribution is MANDATORY — make it a law.             ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
