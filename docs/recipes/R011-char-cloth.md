# R011 — Char Cloth (Pyrolysed Cotton / Plant Fibre)

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R011 · CHAR CLOTH                                       ║
║  End product: 10 pieces char cloth, 4×4 cm each                 ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHAT YOU GET                                                    ║
║    Jet-black, fragile fabric that ignites from a single spark    ║
║    at ~300°C. Coal spreads uniformly. Remains glowing 60+ sec.  ║
║    Failure point: none if fully charred and kept dry.           ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS                                                     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) 15 pieces plant fibre cloth, 4×4 cm, ~5g total             ║
║     Options (in order of preference):                            ║
║     1. Linen cloth (from flax — [→ R013])                       ║
║     2. Cotton cloth (if available — trade good)                  ║
║     3. Dried, flattened cattail leaf (Typha) — grows on Nile    ║
║     4. Dried pounded papyrus inner pith                          ║
║     5. Dried mushroom (amadou fungus, Fomes fomentarius) —       ║
║        inner flesh; grows on dead trees. No processing needed.  ║
║     Condition: bone dry before charring.                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) Any heat source able to sustain 350–400°C         [→ R010] ║
║     A small campfire, forge fire, or even a clay oil lamp works. ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • 1 small clay pot with tight-fitting clay lid               ║
║      OR: a folded piece of hardwood bark (birch, willow)        ║
║      sealed at edges with wet clay. Any container that:         ║
║      (a) holds the cloth, (b) allows steam/gas to escape via    ║
║          a small vent hole, (c) does NOT let air reach cloth.   ║
║    • A small nail, thorn, or stick for vent hole: 3 mm dia.    ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  CONDITIONS                                                      ║
║    • Temperature: 300–450°C (orange-red charcoal, not flame)    ║
║    • Atmosphere: REDUCED OXYGEN inside the pot (this is key).   ║
║      If cloth gets air, it burns to ash. The vent hole lets     ║
║      steam and pyrolysis gases OUT but keeps air from getting IN.║
║    • Time: 5–10 minutes at temperature.                          ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — PREPARE CONTAINER                                      ║
║    1a. Use a small clay pot (~500 mL). Poke one hole in the lid  ║
║        with a thorn: 3 mm diameter. This is the vent.           ║
║    1b. If no pot: wrap cloth loosely in bark, fold into packet,  ║
║        seal all edges with pressed wet clay except one tiny gap. ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — LOAD                                                   ║
║    2a. Fold cloth pieces loosely (not tightly compressed).       ║
║    2b. Place all 15 pieces into pot. Fill pot ½ to ¾ full.      ║
║    2c. Seal lid firmly. Clay-seal the lid joint with wet clay.   ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — HEAT                                                   ║
║    3a. Place sealed pot directly into burning charcoal or on     ║
║        a bed of hot coals. NOT in open flame (too hot → ash).   ║
║    3b. Watch the vent hole. It will emit:                        ║
║        — First 2 min: white steam (moisture leaving cloth).      ║
║        — Next 3–5 min: white then yellow smoke (pyrolysis gases).║
║        — Final state: smoke stops. Vent emits nothing.          ║
║    3c. When smoke stops completely: cloth is fully charred.      ║
║        Remove immediately. Do NOT wait — it may start burning.  ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 4 — COOL AND CHECK                                         ║
║    4a. Set pot aside. Do NOT open for 10 minutes (cloth is hot  ║
║        and will ignite if exposed to air while still glowing).  ║
║    4b. After 10 min: open. Cloth should be jet black, uniform.  ║
║    4c. REJECT any pieces that are:                               ║
║        — Brown (not fully charred — redo these)                  ║
║        — Ash-grey powder (too hot, oxygen got in — discard)      ║
║        — Exactly black but crumbles easily → perfect            ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 5 — STORE                                                  ║
║    5a. Store char cloth in a sealed clay pot or tight leather    ║
║        pouch. Away from moisture.                                ║
║    5b. Properly stored char cloth lasts years.                   ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  SKILLS                                                          ║
║    • Watching the vent hole — patience required.                 ║
║    • Timing removal: too early = brown cloth (poor ignition);    ║
║      too late (or if pot cracks) = ash.                          ║
║    TRAINING TIME: 1 batch practice to master.                    ║
║    CYCLE TIME: 15–20 min per batch of 15 cloths.                ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  YIELD AND SCALING                                               ║
║    • 1 batch (15 cloths) = 2-week fire supply for 1 person.     ║
║    • 1 person can produce 6 batches/day = 90 char cloths.       ║
║    • PROPHET'S NOTE: Set up a char-cloth production station in   ║
║      Year 1 Week 1. 1 worker produces enough for 90 fire kits   ║
║      per day. Fire kits distributed to ALL team leaders          ║
║      immediately — reliability of fire = reliability of all      ║
║      downstream processes.                                       ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  ALTERNATIVE: AMADOU FUNGUS (no processing required)            ║
║    Fomes fomentarius (tinder conk) grows on dead sycamore, ash, ║
║    birch trees. Cut inner spongy layer (brown, felt-like).       ║
║    This amadou layer catches sparks DIRECTLY — no charring.     ║
║    Lasts: 1 fungus = 10–50 ignitions. Collect wherever found.   ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
