# R020 — Basic Clay Pottery (Hand-Built Vessel)

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R020 · BASIC CLAY POTTERY                               ║
║  End product: 1 fired clay pot, 2–5 L capacity, water-tight     ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHAT YOU GET                                                    ║
║    A terracotta vessel able to: hold water, boil water over fire,║
║    store grain, serve as kiln crucible (at basic temperatures).  ║
║    Fired temp: 850–950°C → stoneware-equivalent hardness.       ║
║    Water tightness: improved by burnishing before firing.        ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS                                                     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) 3 kg raw refractory clay             [→ R000-G]  ■ TERM.   ║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) 500g grog (crushed fired pottery)    [→ RECYCLE]            ║
║     Grog = ground-up old fired pots, 1–3 mm grain size.         ║
║     First batch: use coarse sand (500g) instead — river sand     ║
║     with 1–3 mm grains.                             ■ TERM.     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  C) Water — enough to achieve plasticity [→ R000-H]  ■ TERM.   ║
║     Approx. 300–400 mL per 3 kg clay.                           ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • Smooth river pebble (for burnishing), 5 cm dia.  ■ TERM.  ║
║    • Flat board or clean flat stone (work surface)    ■ TERM.   ║
║    • Thin stick or bone tool (for smoothing inside)   ■ TERM.   ║
║    • Campfire or kiln                               [→ R021]    ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  CONDITIONS                                                      ║
║    • Clay preparation: wedge until smooth, no air pockets        ║
║    • Drying: SLOW drying prevents cracking                       ║
║      - 24h in shade (not sun) — sun dries too fast, cracks form  ║
║      - Then 24h in sun before firing                             ║
║    • Firing: temperature must reach 850°C minimum               ║
║    • Heating rate: slow! 50°C per 10 minutes up to 600°C         ║
║      (steam explodes trapped moisture above 100°C if heated fast)║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — PREPARE CLAY BODY                                      ║
║    1a. Weigh 3 kg raw clay + 500g grog/sand.                     ║
║    1b. Mix dry on flat stone. Break all clay lumps.              ║
║    1c. Add water slowly: 50 mL at a time. Mix after each        ║
║        addition. Total ~350 mL. Target: clay holds a shape       ║
║        pressed by thumb but doesn't stick to hands.              ║
║    1d. WEDGE: press clay down with heel of palm, fold in half,  ║
║        rotate 90°, repeat. 10 minutes (100+ wedging motions).    ║
║        Look for: smooth surface, no visible air bubble pockets. ║
║    1e. Test: cut clay ball in half with wire or string. Cross-   ║
║        section should show no bubbles. If bubbles: wedge more.  ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — BUILD THE POT (coil method)                            ║
║    2a. Pinch base: take 300g clay, press between palms to flat   ║
║        disc 15 cm diameter, 1.5 cm thick. Smooth on flat board. ║
║    2b. Roll coils: take 100g portions, roll on flat surface to   ║
║        1.5 cm diameter × 30 cm long snakes.                      ║
║    2c. Coil-build walls: place first coil on base edge. Press    ║
║        down inside with thumb, smear upward to bond to base.     ║
║        Outside: smooth with wet fingers downward.                ║
║    2d. Continue coil by coil, pressing and smoothing each.       ║
║        Wall thickness target: 7–9 mm uniform.                    ║
║    2e. For a 3 L pot: build 8 cm tall walls, 15 cm base dia.    ║
║        flaring slightly outward to 18 cm at mid-height, then    ║
║        narrowing to 12 cm neck. Total height: 20 cm.            ║
║    2f. Rim: press top coil flat, smooth with wet finger inside   ║
║        and outside. Run thumb around inside rim to create slight ║
║        bevel (helps fitting a lid).                              ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — BURNISH (optional but important)                       ║
║    3a. Let pot stiffen in shade ~4 hours (leather-hard = holds   ║
║        shape but still cool to touch, slight give to thumbnail). ║
║    3b. Use smooth river pebble to burnish outside surface:       ║
║        rub in firm circular strokes until surface is smooth and  ║
║        shiny. Burnishing aligns clay particles → seals micro-pores║
║        → better water-tightness after firing.                    ║
║    3c. For better seal: apply thin slip (clay + water, cream     ║
║        consistency) to outside, let stiffen, then burnish again. ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 4 — DRY                                                    ║
║    4a. Place in shade. NOT sun, NOT by fire. 24 hours minimum.  ║
║    4b. Rotate pot every 4 hours for even drying.                 ║
║    4c. After 24h: move to gentle sun. 12 more hours.            ║
║    4d. TEST: press thumbnail into thickest wall. Leaves NO       ║
║        mark = bone dry = ready to fire.                          ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 5 — FIRE (open fire, campfire method)                      ║
║    5a. Preheat phase: place pot beside fire (not in it). 30 min. ║
║        Any remaining moisture leaves slowly.                     ║
║    5b. Nest the pot in embers, surrounding with fuel (dry acacia ║
║        wood). Cover with more fuel. Light.                       ║
║    5c. Visual guide: pot will glow faint red at ~600°C.         ║
║        Orange-red = 800–900°C. Maintain 90–120 min.             ║
║    5d. Cool: DO NOT remove from embers immediately. Let fire     ║
║        die naturally. Remove after 2 hours. Never quench in     ║
║        water (thermal shock cracks the pot).                     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 6 — TEST AND SEAL                                          ║
║    6a. Tap rim with knuckle: clear ring = fully fired and no     ║
║        cracks. Dull thud = crack or under-fired.                 ║
║    6b. Optional waterproofing: boil pine resin or acacia gum     ║
║        inside filled pot for 20 min → resin fills micro-pores.  ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  SKILLS                                                          ║
║    • Wedging: 30 minutes practice                                ║
║    • Coil building: 2–3 hours to produce first serviceable pot  ║
║    • Firing temperature judgment: practice with scrap pieces     ║
║    TRAINING TIME: 1 day for basic pot; 1 week for consistency.  ║
║    CYCLE TIME: 3 hours making + 48 hours drying + 3 hours firing ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PRODUCTION SCALING                                              ║
║    • 1 trained potter: 5–8 pots per day (making phase).         ║
║    • Fire 50 pots per batch in a pit kiln [→ R021].             ║
║    • Target: 500 pots in first month (100 workers' household     ║
║      supply, water storage for smelting operation).             ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
