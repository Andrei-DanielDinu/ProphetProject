# R004 — Antler and Hardwood Mining Picks (Non-Metal Mining)

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R004 · NON-METAL MINING PICKS & THERMAL SPALLING        ║
║  End product: 1 × Antler Tine Pick + Thermal Splitting Protocol ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  THE PROBLEM                                                     ║
║    You need copper and iron ore to make metal tools.             ║
║    However, the ore is locked inside solid bedrock!              ║
║    How do you extract 1,000 kg of rock before you own a pickaxe? ║
║    Answer: Fire-setting, wooden expansion wedges, and antler     ║
║    lever-picks.                                                  ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS                                                     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) 1 × Red deer or gazelle antler beam with brow tine           ║
║     [→ R000-K] ■ TERMINAL. Dense horn core, Mohs 4, shock-proof.║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) 10 × Dry acacia wood wedges (15 cm long × 4 cm wide × 2 cm tap)║
║     [→ R000-I] ■ TERMINAL. Bone-dry (baked near fire).           ║
║                                                                  ║
║  C) 50 kg Dry firewood (for thermal fracturing) [→ R000-I]       ║
║                                                                  ║
║  D) 40 L Water (for rapid thermal quenching) [→ R000-H]          ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • Quartzite mining maul (R001)                                ║
║    • Water skin / clay bucket (R020)                             ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  METHOD 1 — THERMAL FIRE-SETTING (Primary extraction method)     ║
║    1a. Pile dry firewood tight against the exposed rock ore face.║
║    1b. Light fire (R010) and burn vigorously for 2–4 hours until ║
║        the rock surface reaches 600–700°C.                       ║
║    1c. Quartz and minerals expand differentially, creating       ║
║        immense internal shear stress.                            ║
║    1d. Splash buckets of cold water directly onto the glowing    ║
║        rock face.                                                ║
║    1e. Sudden contraction causes explosive spalling: rock cracks ║
║        with loud pistol-shot sounds, fracturing into 5–20 cm     ║
║        slabs.                                                    ║
║                                                                  ║
║  METHOD 2 — HYDRAULIC WOODEN SPLITTING WEDGES                    ║
║    2a. Find the newly formed thermal fissures or natural joints. ║
║    2b. Drive bone-dry acacia wedges (B) deep into fissures using ║
║        the heavy stone maul (R001).                              ║
║    2c. Pour water over the wooden wedges continuously.           ║
║    2d. Wood fibers absorb water through capillary action and     ║
║        swell laterally, exerting up to 10 MPa (1,450 psi)        ║
║        expansive pressure against the fissure walls.             ║
║    2e. Within 30–60 minutes, the rock cleaves open along the seam.║
║                                                                  ║
║  METHOD 3 — ANTLER PICK PRYING                                   ║
║    3a. Insert brow tine of antler pick into loosened seams.      ║
║    3b. Use antler beam as a class-1 lever, pulling with body     ║
║        weight against stone fulcrum.                             ║
║    3c. Pry ore chunks free and shovel into carry baskets.        ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  DAILY EXTRACTION RATE                                           ║
║    A 4-person mining team using thermal fire-setting can quarry  ║
║    500–800 kg of rock per day without a single scrap of metal.   ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
