# R030 — Copper Smelting (Malachite → Pure Copper Ingot)

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R030 · COPPER SMELTING                                   ║
║  End product: 10 kg copper ingot, >98% Cu, castable             ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHAT YOU GET                                                    ║
║    Pure copper metal. Colour: bright orange-red on fresh fracture.║
║    Density: 8.9 g/cm³ (heavy — weight test: 10 kg ingot is small,║
║    ~1.1 L volume). Hardness: HV 40–60 (soft — bend with hands). ║
║    Use: cast tools, wire, alloying with tin (→ bronze, R031).   ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS (per 10 kg copper output)                           ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) 50 kg pre-crushed malachite ore, 20–40% Cu [→ R000-C] TERM.║
║     Math: 50 kg ore × 30% Cu avg = 15 kg Cu mineral available.  ║
║     Minus 33% losses (slag, unextracted) = ~10 kg Cu output.    ║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) 25 kg charcoal                              [→ R025]        ║
║     Ratio: 2.5 kg charcoal per 1 kg copper output. Charcoal     ║
║     both FUELS the fire AND provides CO reducing atmosphere      ║
║     (Cu₂O + CO → 2Cu + CO₂).                                    ║
║                                                                  ║
║  C) 5 kg calcined limestone (CaO flux)          [→ R027]        ║
║     Ratio: 1 kg CaO per 10 kg ore. CaO reacts with silica        ║
║     gangue: CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃ (calcium silicate slag).        ║
║     Slag forms a liquid layer above copper pool → easy separation.║
║                                                                  ║
║  D) 10 L water (tapping, cooling, emergency)    [→ R000-H] TERM.║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • 1 updraft mudbrick kiln (the furnace)       [→ R021]       ║
║    • 2 pot bellows (continuous forced draft)      [→ R026]       ║
║    • Clay tuyere pipe, 3 cm ID × 40 cm long       [→ R020]      ║
║      Inserted through furnace wall 10 cm above base.             ║
║    • Granite stone mold (pre-carved ingot channel):              ║
║      Carved: 30 cm × 10 cm × 3 cm channel in flat granite slab. ║
║      Pre-heat to 200°C before pouring (prevents cold shut).      ║
║    • Clay plug (for tapping hole in furnace base)                ║
║    • Long iron (later) or hardwood pole for stirring             ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  CONDITIONS                                                      ║
║    • Target temperature: 1,100–1,150°C (copper melts at 1,083°C)║
║    • Visual: white-orange glow visible at tuyere zone.           ║
║    • Atmosphere: REDUCING (excess charcoal = CO-rich). If air   ║
║      excess occurs, copper reoxidises back to Cu₂O (green).     ║
║    • Bellows: continuous. Never stop during active smelt.        ║
║    • Tapping: perform within 5 min of reaching full temperature. ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  FURNACE CONFIGURATION FOR COPPER SMELTING                       ║
║                                                                  ║
║  Cross-section of kiln modified for copper smelting:            ║
║                                                                  ║
║    ════════════════  ← flue (top draft)                          ║
║    │                │                                            ║
║    │  charge zone   │  ← ore + charcoal charge (60 cm tall)     ║
║    │                │                                            ║
║    │────────────────│  ← tuyere pipe enters HERE (10 cm above   ║
║    │   ► TUYERE     │    base); angle: 20° downward             ║
║    │                │                                            ║
║    │  copper pool   │  ← liquid Cu pools at base (1,083°C)      ║
║    │  slag layer    │  ← slag floats on Cu (less dense)          ║
║    ╘════╤═══════════╛                                            ║
║         │ tap hole (3 cm dia, 5 cm from base)                    ║
║         └────────────► granite ingot mold (pre-heated)           ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — ORE PREPARATION (Day -1 or Morning)                    ║
║    1a. Crush malachite: use 2 kg quartzite cobble as hammer.    ║
║        Strike chunks on flat stone anvil. Break to <2 cm pieces. ║
║        Rate: 1 worker = 100 kg crushed per day.                  ║
║    1b. BENEFICIATE: hand-sort ore pile. Keep bright green, blue- ║
║        green, turquoise pieces. Discard: white, grey, brown rock ║
║        (gangue). Target: green fraction ≥50% of pile weight.     ║
║    1c. Roast ore: spread 50 kg in flat layer on flat stone.     ║
║        Heat over open fire (600°C, 1 hour). Colour shifts from  ║
║        green → black (Cu₂O forms). This:                         ║
║        • Drives off water (reduces steam explosion risk)         ║
║        • Breaks up ore further (thermal cracking)               ║
║        • Converts some CuCO₃ → CuO (easier to reduce)          ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — FURNACE PREPARATION                                    ║
║    2a. Inspect kiln from previous firing. Repair any cracks in  ║
║        refractory lining with fresh mix (R023).                  ║
║    2b. Seal tap hole at base with clay plug (3 cm dia, 5 cm     ║
║        deep). Clay plug must be dry before firing starts.        ║
║    2c. Preheat furnace: light small fire in stoke hole. 30 min  ║
║        at 200°C (drives moisture from lining → prevents steam    ║
║        explosion during main smelt).                             ║
║    2d. Pre-heat stone ingot mold beside furnace: place in        ║
║        embers for 30 min → 200°C.                                ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — CHARGE LOADING                                         ║
║    Load in alternating layers from the bottom:                   ║
║    Layer 1 (bottom):  5 cm charcoal (~2 kg)                      ║
║    Layer 2:           10 cm crushed ore (~10 kg) + 1 kg CaO flux║
║    Layer 3:           5 cm charcoal (~2 kg)                      ║
║    Layer 4:           10 cm ore + 1 kg CaO                       ║
║    Continue: 5 layers ore + flux / 5 layers charcoal.            ║
║    Total column height: 60–70 cm.                                ║
║    Total charge: 25 kg charcoal + 50 kg ore + 5 kg CaO.        ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 4 — IGNITION AND BELLOWS (90–120 min total)               ║
║    4a. Light base of charge through stoke hole with fire brand.  ║
║    4b. Allow natural draft 10 min. Ore begins to glow at base.  ║
║    4c. Begin bellows: 2 operators, alternating pot bellows.      ║
║        Rate: 1 stroke/second per pot.                            ║
║    4d. Temperature progression (observe tuyere zone colour):     ║
║        — 20 min: dark red (~650°C)                               ║
║        — 40 min: bright red (~800°C)                             ║
║        — 60 min: orange-red (~950°C)                             ║
║        — 80 min: orange-white (~1,050°C)                         ║
║        — 90 min: white-orange glow → 1,100°C → TAP NOW          ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 5 — TAPPING                                                ║
║    5a. When white-orange achieved: prepare ingot mold (handle    ║
║        with thick wet leather or doubled cloth — it's at 200°C). ║
║    5b. Using a hardwood or stone rod: punch out the clay tap     ║
║        plug from the base hole. Stand to the SIDE, not in front  ║
║        (molten copper and slag may spray).                        ║
║    5c. Copper (more dense, 8.9 g/cm³) flows first. Slag (less    ║
║        dense, ~2.5 g/cm³) follows. Watch colour:                 ║
║        — Bright orange liquid = copper                           ║
║        — Dark red/black viscous = slag                           ║
║    5d. Collect copper in pre-heated granite mold. When slag      ║
║        appears: divert to slag pit (bare earth is fine).         ║
║    5e. Continue bellows WHILE tapping.                           ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 6 — COOLING AND INSPECTION                                 ║
║    6a. Allow ingot to cool 20 min before touching (still >500°C  ║
║        after 10 min).                                            ║
║    6b. When surface darkens (black oxide layer forms): quench    ║
║        ingot in bucket of water (copper tolerates this — unlike  ║
║        steel).                                                   ║
║    6c. Inspect: bright orange-red fracture = pure Cu. Green      ║
║        patches = copper oxide inclusions (reduce next run with   ║
║        more charcoal).                                           ║
║    6d. Density test: weigh ingot, measure water displacement.   ║
║        Result should be 8.5–9.0 g/cm³.                          ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  YIELD AND SCALING                                               ║
║    Per run: 50 kg ore → ~10 kg Cu ingot. 2 hrs active smelting. ║
║    Daily capacity (2 kilns, 2 runs/day): 40 kg Cu/day.          ║
║    Weekly target Year 1: 200 kg Cu/week (5 kilns operational).  ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  SKILLS                                                          ║
║    • Charge loading: 1 hour instruction.                         ║
║    • Temperature reading by visual: 2–3 practice runs.          ║
║    • Tapping: requires calm and speed simultaneously. 5 runs to ║
║      master. NEVER rush — spilled molten copper is catastrophic. ║
║    TRAINING TIME: 1 week to reliable production team.            ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  SAFETY (CRITICAL)                                               ║
║    ⚠ Molten copper at 1,100°C causes immediate full-thickness    ║
║      burns. Minimum safe distance from tap hole: 2 m.            ║
║    ⚠ NEVER pour into wet or cold molds — steam explosion.        ║
║      Preheat ALL molds. Keep water bucket away from pour zone.  ║
║    ⚠ Copper fumes (CuO) are toxic. Work CROSSWIND of furnace.   ║
║    ⚠ Stone ingot molds crack if thermal shocked — preheat always.║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
