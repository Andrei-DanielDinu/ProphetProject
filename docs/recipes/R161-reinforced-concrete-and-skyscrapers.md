# R161 — Reinforced Concrete and High-Rise Structural Framing

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R161 · REINFORCED CONCRETE & HIGH-RISE FRAMEWORK         ║
║  End product: C35 Structural Column & Slab (Skyscraper Skeleton) ║
║  Compressive: 35 MPa (Concrete) / Tensile: 450 MPa (Steel Rebar) ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  THE COMPOSITE MIRACLE OF REINFORCED CONCRETE                    ║
║    Plain concrete has tremendous compressive strength (supports  ║
║    weight), but weak tensile strength (cracks under bending).    ║
║    Steel has immense tensile strength, but buckles under weight. ║
║    Together, they form a perfect synergy:                        ║
║    1. Steel rebar handles 100% of the tensile and shear forces.  ║
║    2. Concrete carries 100% of compressive gravity loads.        ║
║    3. Coefficient of thermal expansion is IDENTICAL:             ║
║       Concrete α ≈ 12 × 10⁻⁶ / °C; Steel α ≈ 12 × 10⁻⁶ / °C.      ║
║       They expand and contract together without debonding!       ║
║    4. Alkaline concrete chemistry (pH > 12.5) passivates steel,  ║
║       permanently preventing rust!                               ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  CONCRETE BATCH PROPORTIONS (per 1 m³ C35 structural mix)       ║
║  A) 380 kg Ordinary Portland Cement [→ R160]                     ║
║  B) 160 L Clean Water [→ R000-H] (Water/Cement ratio = 0.42)     ║
║  C) 750 kg Sharp Quartz Sand (0.1–2 mm fine aggregate) [→ R000-F]║
║  D) 1,150 kg Crushed Granite/Basalt (5–20 mm coarse aggregate)   ║
║     [→ R000-Mine / R001: mechanically crushed in jaw crusher]   ║
║  E) 120 kg Deformed High-Yield Steel Rebar (Grade 60) [→ R041]   ║
║     (Deformed ribs hot-rolled onto bars to mechanically lock     ║
║     into hardened concrete matrix).                              ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  STRUCTURAL SKYSCRAPER COLUMN FABRICATION                        ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — REBAR CAGE TYING                                       ║
║    1a. Four corner vertical bars: 25 mm dia high-tensile steel.  ║
║    1b. Lateral stirrups/ties: 8 mm steel wire bent into square   ║
║        hoops every 15 cm along the height (prevents vertical     ║
║        bars from buckling under immense axial skyscraper loads). ║
║    1c. Tie junctions with annealed iron binding wire (R084).     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — FORMWORK & EMBEDMENT                                   ║
║    2a. Erect rigid timber formwork (R000-I) around cage.         ║
║    2b. Concrete spacers: ensure minimum 40 mm concrete cover     ║
║        between outer formwork and rebar (protects steel from     ║
║        external fire heat and moisture).                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — POURING & MECHANICAL VIBRATION                         ║
║    3a. Mix cement, water, sand, and aggregate in rotating drum.  ║
║    3b. Pour continuous lift into column formwork.                ║
║    3c. Immersion needle vibrator (10,000 VPM, R080):             ║
║        Vibrate concrete for 15 seconds per insertion point.      ║
║        Trapped air bubbles escape to surface; slurry liquefies   ║
║        and packs dense around every rebar tooth with zero voids. ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 4 — HYDRATION CURING PROTOCOL (Non-Optional!)              ║
║    4a. Concrete does NOT "dry" — it CHEMICALLY HYDRATES!         ║
║        If water evaporates early, strength drops by 60%!         ║
║    4b. Keep formwork wet and covered with wet burlap for 14 days.║
║    4c. Day 7: 70% strength (25 MPa). Day 28: 100% design (38 MPa)║
║        Stripping formwork allows stacking the next floor slab!   ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
