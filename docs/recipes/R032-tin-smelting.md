# R032 — Tin Metal (Cassiterite Smelting)

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R032 · TIN SMELTING (Cassiterite → Tin Metal)           ║
║  End product: 3 kg pure tin, >97% Sn                           ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHAT YOU GET                                                    ║
║    Pure tin metal. Colour: bright silver-white. Melts at 232°C  ║
║    (you can melt it on a fire without bellows). Soft (HV 5).    ║
║    Alloys with copper to make bronze [→ R031].                  ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  CRITICAL SUPPLY PROBLEM — READ FIRST                            ║
║                                                                  ║
║    ⚠ EGYPT HAS NO PRIMARY TIN DEPOSITS.                          ║
║                                                                  ║
║    Tin must come from one of these sources:                      ║
║    Option A: Trade with Anatolia (Turkey)                        ║
║      Taurus Mountains — 37°N 34°E. 1,500 km from Memphis.      ║
║      Route: Memphis → Sinai → Levant coast → Anatolia.          ║
║      Journey: 2–3 month sea/land caravan. Establish in Year 2.  ║
║                                                                  ║
║    Option B: Afghan cassiterite (later trade route)              ║
║      Badakhshan — 36.7°N 70.8°E. 4,500 km. Too far initially.  ║
║      Historically the Bronze Age tin source for Egypt.           ║
║                                                                  ║
║    Option C: European cassiterite (Phase 3 trade)               ║
║      Cornwall, England — 50.3°N 5.1°W. Far but richest.         ║
║                                                                  ║
║    PROPHET'S IMMEDIATE ACTION (Year 1, Month 2):                ║
║      Dispatch a 10-person trade expedition to Levant with:       ║
║      — 20 kg blue glass beads (product already demonstrable)    ║
║      — 10 kg copper ingot (Egypt has copper; they want tin)     ║
║      Goal: return with 100 kg cassiterite or tin metal.         ║
║      This establishes the trade route for all future bronze.    ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS (per 3 kg tin output)                               ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) 5 kg cassiterite ore (SnO₂)   [■ TERMINAL — mine/trade]    ║
║     Math: SnO₂ MW=150.7, Sn MW=118.7 → 78.8% Sn by weight.    ║
║     Real ore grade: 50–70% SnO₂. At 60% SnO₂: 60%×78.8%=47%  ║
║     Sn in ore. 5 kg × 47% × 85% recovery = ~2.0 kg Sn.        ║
║     Adjust ore quantity to hit 3 kg output target.              ║
║                                                                  ║
║     FIELD IDENTIFICATION of cassiterite:                         ║
║     1. Colour: brown-black to black. Lustrous (adamantine luster).║
║     2. Density: VERY heavy — 6.8–7.1 g/cm³ (comparable to iron ║
║        ore, much heavier than most rock). "Heavy for its size"  ║
║        is the key field sign.                                    ║
║     3. Streak: white to grey (pale).                             ║
║     4. Hardness: Mohs 6–7 (scratches glass but scratched by     ║
║        quartz/flint).                                            ║
║     5. Habit: short prismatic crystals in granitic or pegmatitic ║
║        veins, or as rounded "stream tin" (placer) in riverbeds. ║
║     6. NOT magnetic (unlike magnetite which is similarly dark   ║
║        and dense). Test with iron wire.                          ║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) 5 kg charcoal (fuel + reductant)      [→ R025]             ║
║     Reduction: SnO₂ + 2C → Sn + 2CO↑ at 1,000°C.             ║
║                                                                  ║
║  C) 500g calcined limestone (CaO)         [→ R027]             ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • Updraft kiln or small crucible furnace        [→ R021]    ║
║    • Pot bellows                                   [→ R026]    ║
║    • 2 L clay crucible                             [→ R033]    ║
║    • Granite ingot mold                            [■ TERM.]   ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  CONDITIONS                                                      ║
║    • Temperature: 1,000–1,100°C sustained (cassiterite reduces  ║
║      more easily than copper ore — doesn't need 1,100°C min).  ║
║    • Tin melts at 232°C: it pools at base easily.               ║
║    • Atmosphere: reducing (CO-rich from excess charcoal).        ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — ORE PREP                                               ║
║    1a. Crush cassiterite to 1–2 cm pieces. No roasting needed.  ║
║    1b. If placer (stream) tin: already fine-grained. Wash with   ║
║        water to remove lighter sand/clay (cassiterite settles    ║
║        faster due to density — pan same as gold panning).        ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — LOAD CRUCIBLE                                          ║
║    2a. Layer: 1 cm charcoal / 2 cm cassiterite / 1 cm CaO.     ║
║    2b. Repeat. Fill crucible 80%.                                ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — SMELT                                                  ║
║    3a. Set crucible in kiln with charcoal surround.             ║
║    3b. Apply bellows. Reach 1,000–1,100°C (orange-red).         ║
║    3c. Hold 60 min. Tin reduces and pools at crucible base       ║
║        (lighter slag floats above).                              ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 4 — POUR                                                   ║
║    4a. Remove crucible with tongs. Carefully pour off slag       ║
║        (top layer) into slag pit.                                ║
║    4b. Pour tin (bottom layer) into pre-heated granite mold.    ║
║    4c. Tin at 232°C melts point — even if slightly cooled       ║
║        during pour it re-pools. Very forgiving metal.            ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  SKILLS: Same as copper smelting. Easier due to lower temp.     ║
║  CYCLE TIME: 90 min per batch, 3 kg tin output.                 ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
