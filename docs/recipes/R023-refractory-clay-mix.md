# R023 — Refractory Clay Lining Mix

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R023 · REFRACTORY CLAY LINING MIX                       ║
║  End product: 50 kg refractory mix, withstands 1,300°C          ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHAT YOU GET                                                    ║
║    A castable/trowellable mix that, once fired, forms a dense    ║
║    refractory lining able to withstand repeated thermal cycling  ║
║    to 1,300°C without cracking or melting. Used for: kiln        ║
║    interior, bloomery furnace inner wall, forge hearth,          ║
║    copper/iron smelting crucibles.                               ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS (50 kg batch = lines ~2 m² at 5 cm depth)          ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) 25 kg kaolin-rich refractory clay (dry weight) [→ R000-G]  ║
║     White or buff colour. High Al₂O₃ content. Mohs 1–2 raw,    ║
║     but fired product reaches Mohs 6–7.                          ║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) 15 kg coarse quartz sand, 1–3 mm grain  [→ R000-F] TERM.  ║
║     Provides skeletal structure, reduces shrinkage cracking.    ║
║     Wash before use: pour into bucket, agitate, discard floaters.║
║                                                                  ║
║  C) 7.5 kg grog (ground fired pottery)      [RECYCLE]           ║
║     Crush old fired pots with stone hammer → 1–5 mm pieces.     ║
║     Grog is pre-fired: it doesn't shrink → prevents crack-forming║
║     during the first kiln firing of the lining.                  ║
║     First batch: use coarse crushed quartzite gravel instead.    ║
║                                                                  ║
║  D) 2.5 kg chopped dry straw or grass fibres [→ R000-I] TERM.  ║
║     Length: 1–3 cm pieces. Fibres burn out on first firing,     ║
║     leaving microscopic channels → better thermal shock resist. ║
║                                                                  ║
║  E) Water — to mix to workable consistency   [→ R000-H] TERM.  ║
║     Approx. 15–20 L per 50 kg dry mix.                          ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║    1. Combine all dry ingredients (A–D). Mix thoroughly dry.     ║
║    2. Add water gradually (500 mL at a time). Mix between        ║
║       each addition. Target: stiff clay dough — holds shape      ║
║       when formed into ball, slightly sticky to touch.           ║
║    3. Use immediately (mixture stiffens over 2–4 hours if left). ║
║    4. Apply to surface to be lined: 3 cm layer (first coat).    ║
║    5. Compress firmly by hand/wooden paddle. Smooth surface.     ║
║    6. Allow to firm (not fully dry) — 2–4 hours.                ║
║    7. Apply second coat: 2 cm. Total lining: 5 cm.             ║
║    8. Smooth interior surface with wet hand.                     ║
║    9. Allow 48 hours in shade to set before first fire.          ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PERFORMANCE AFTER CURING                                        ║
║    • Maximum service temperature: 1,300°C (sustained)           ║
║    • Thermal cycles before replacement: 50–100 (then re-line     ║
║      damaged areas).                                             ║
║    • Bonding to mudbrick: good (compatible clay materials).     ║
║    • Water resistance: poor if not fired — do not use in         ║
║      standing water. Good after repeated firing.                ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  SKILLS: Basic mixing. No special technique. 1 hour to learn.   ║
║  CYCLE TIME: 2 hours mixing + 48 hours setting                  ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
