# R060 — Soda-Lime Glass (Batch Melting)

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R060 · SODA-LIME GLASS BATCH                            ║
║  End product: 10 kg molten glass batch, ready for forming       ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHAT YOU GET                                                    ║
║    Molten glass at 1,100°C. After forming and cooling:          ║
║    — Transparent (green-tinted without MnO₂ decolorant)        ║
║    — Coloured with metal oxide additions (see table)             ║
║    — Mohs 5.5 (can be scratched by flint but not by copper)     ║
║    — Non-porous, chemically stable                               ║
║    — Can be ground to optical flatness                           ║
║    Uses: vessels, windows, lenses (R061), beads (trade good).  ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS (per 10 kg glass batch)                            ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) 7.5 kg silica sand, washed, fine       [→ R000-F] ■ TERM.  ║
║     Pure white quartz sand. Iron content <0.1% for clear glass. ║
║     Grain size: 0.1–0.5 mm (sieve through woven cloth).         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) 2.5 kg natron, anhydrous equivalent    [→ R000-E] ■ TERM.  ║
║     (= 6.9 kg hydrated natron Na₂CO₃·10H₂O)                    ║
║     Flux: lowers silica melting point from 1,713°C to ~1,000°C. ║
║     Math: Na₂CO₃ MW=106; anhydrous fraction = 106/286 = 37%.   ║
║     So use 6.9 kg hydrated natron to get 2.5 kg Na₂CO₃.        ║
║                                                                  ║
║  C) 1 kg calcined limestone (CaO)          [→ R027]             ║
║     Glass stabiliser. Without CaO: glass dissolves slowly in    ║
║     water (soluble silicate glass — useless for vessels).        ║
║     CaO content in final glass: ~10%.                           ║
║                                                                  ║
║  D) OPTIONAL COLOURANTS (add to base batch, see table)          ║
║     All amounts per 10 kg base batch:                           ║
║     ┌─────────────────┬────────────┬──────────────────────────┐ ║
║     │ Colourant       │ Amount     │ Source                   │ ║
║     ├─────────────────┼────────────┼──────────────────────────┤ ║
║     │ Cobalt oxide    │ 10–50g     │ Cobaltite (Eastern Desert)│ ║
║     │ (CoO)           │            │ → intense royal blue     │ ║
║     │ Copper oxide    │ 50g (green)│ Copper smelt scale/dross  │ ║
║     │ (CuO)           │ 10g (blue) │ → green or turquoise     │ ║
║     │ Iron oxide      │ 50–200g    │ Red hematite powder      │ ║
║     │ (Fe₂O₃)         │            │ → amber to brown         │ ║
║     │ Manganese       │ 50g        │ Pyrolusite (Eastern Des.) │ ║
║     │ dioxide (MnO₂)  │            │ → decolourant (removes   │ ║
║     │                 │            │   green iron tint)       │ ║
║     └─────────────────┴────────────┴──────────────────────────┘ ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • Glass furnace (specialised clay crucible furnace)  [→ R062]║
║    • Clay crucible, 15 L capacity                       [→ R033]║
║    • Long clay-coated iron (later) stirring rod         [→ R041]║
║    • Forming tools (see R063 for glassblowing; R064 for cores)  ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  CONDITIONS                                                      ║
║    • Melting temperature: 1,100–1,200°C for homogeneous melt.   ║
║    • Fining (bubble removal): hold at 1,200°C for 30 min        ║
║      WITHOUT stirring. Bubbles rise at ~0.1 mm/sec in glass.   ║
║    • Working temperature: 1,000–1,050°C (glass flows, shapeable).║
║    • Annealing temperature: 500–550°C (relieve internal stresses).║
║    • Cooling rate: SLOW. Max: 50°C/hour below 500°C.            ║
║      Too fast = internal stress = glass shatters unpredictably  ║
║      when tapped or touched. This is why glass must be annealed.║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — PREPARE BATCH                                          ║
║    1a. Grind natron in stone mortar to powder if lumpy. It       ║
║        absorbs moisture and may be caked. Must be dry.           ║
║    1b. Mix all dry ingredients (A, B, C + any colourant D)      ║
║        thoroughly in a dry clay bowl. Use wooden paddle.         ║
║    1c. Load into glass furnace crucible. Tamp down with paddle. ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — MELT                                                   ║
║    2a. Apply bellows to glass furnace [→ R062].                  ║
║    2b. Temperature schedule:                                     ║
║        0–1h: raise to 600°C (natron melts at 851°C, starts to  ║
║               soften. Vigorous CO₂ release from Na₂CO₃ — normal)║
║        1–2h: raise to 1,000°C. Sand begins to dissolve in melt. ║
║        2–3h: raise to 1,100–1,200°C. Full melt achieved.        ║
║    2c. Stir once at 1,100°C with clay rod. Then DO NOT stir.   ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — FINE (bubble removal)                                  ║
║    3a. Hold at 1,200°C for 30 minutes without stirring.          ║
║    3b. Watch for bubbles rising to surface. When surface is calm ║
║        and glassy-looking: fining complete.                      ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 4 — WORK                                                   ║
║    4a. Reduce bellows to lower temp to 1,050°C.                  ║
║    4b. Glass is now workable: gather with blowpipe or tools     ║
║        for forming [→ R063 glassblowing, → R064 core forming].  ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 5 — ANNEAL (every formed piece)                            ║
║    5a. After forming: place piece in a separate annealing oven   ║
║        [→ R065] at 500°C. Hold 30 min.                          ║
║    5b. Cool at 50°C/hour. At 300°C: free-cooling allowed.       ║
║    5c. TEST: tap piece with copper rod. Clear ring = well        ║
║        annealed. Dull sound = stress = anneal again or discard. ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  YIELD: 10 kg batch → 9.5 kg glass (5% loss from fining         ║
║  evaporation and sticking to crucible walls).                   ║
║  CYCLE TIME: 4 hours melt + 2 hours anneal per batch.           ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PRIORITY PRODUCTS (what to make first)                          ║
║  1. BLUE GLASS BEADS (cobalt oxide): trade goods. 500 beads     ║
║     per batch = enormous wealth in 2975 BCE.                    ║
║  2. WINDOW PANES: flat glass cast on stone slab (later: cylinder ║
║     blow method). Improves light in workshops and school rooms.  ║
║  3. LENSES: optical quality (→ R061).                           ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
