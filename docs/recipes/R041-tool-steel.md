# R041 — Tool Steel (Pack Carburisation → Quench → Temper)

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R041 · TOOL STEEL BAR                                    ║
║  End product: 1 × tool steel bar, 0.6–0.8% C, HV 500 (tempered)║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHAT YOU GET                                                    ║
║    Steel bar ready for hardening/tempering. After full           ║
║    treatment: HV 500–550. Can scratch glass (Mohs ~6.5+).       ║
║    Cuts copper easily. Machines bronze. Shapes granite.          ║
║    THIS is the first material significantly better than          ║
║    anything that has existed before — a true leap.              ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS                                                     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) 220g wrought iron bar (5mm × 15mm × 25cm)   [→ R040]       ║
║     Must be consolidated (no visible slag pores). Draw out to   ║
║     5 mm thickness maximum — carburisation limited by diffusion. ║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) 100g charcoal powder (fine, <1mm grains)     [→ R025]       ║
║     Carburising agent. Carbon source. Ground in stone mortar.    ║
║                                                                  ║
║  C) 50g bone char (calcined bone, ground)        [→ R042]       ║
║     Bone char = mix of carbon (~10%) + calcium phosphate (~80%). ║
║     The Ca₃(PO₄)₂ acts as a catalyst, accelerating C diffusion ║
║     by forming an active CO/CO₂ atmosphere more rapidly.         ║
║     Without bone char: add extra 50g charcoal powder.           ║
║                                                                  ║
║  D) 500g refractory clay (for sealing box)       [→ R023]       ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • 1 clay/ceramic carburisation box, 20×6×6 cm  [→ R033 mod] ║
║      A simple clay box with lid. NOT airtight but tightly fitted.║
║    • Kiln or forge able to hold 900°C             [→ R021]      ║
║    • Brine bucket (10% NaCl solution)             [→ R000-H/L]  ║
║    • Hardwood or iron tongs                       [→ R044]      ║
║    • Sandstone block (for polishing to see temper colours)       ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  CONDITIONS                                                      ║
║    • Carburisation temperature: 900–950°C. Too low (<850°C): slow║
║      Too high (>1,000°C): grain growth (makes steel brittle).   ║
║    • Carburisation time: 8–10 hours for 0.7% C through 5mm bar. ║
║      Rule: ~0.3 mm C penetration per hour at 900°C.             ║
║      5mm bar: C diffuses from BOTH sides: 2.5mm each side = ~8h.║
║    • Quench medium: 10% NaCl brine at 15–25°C. Faster than      ║
║      water, produces harder steel, but more risk of cracking.   ║
║    • Temper temperature: 220°C for cutting tools. Identify by   ║
║      colour on polished surface (see table below).              ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  PHASE 1 — PREPARE CARBURISATION PACK                           ║
║    1a. Mix charcoal powder + bone char 2:1 by weight.            ║
║        (100g charcoal + 50g bone char for one bar.)             ║
║    1b. Place wrought iron bar in clay box.                       ║
║    1c. Pack mixed carburiser tightly around bar on all sides.   ║
║        No air gaps between bar and carburiser.                   ║
║    1d. Seal lid with wet refractory clay (R023). Allow 1h to firm║
║                                                                  ║
║  PHASE 2 — CARBURISE                                             ║
║    2a. Load sealed box into kiln. Surround with charcoal.       ║
║    2b. Fire kiln to 900°C (orange-red in kiln chamber).         ║
║    2c. Hold 8–10 hours at temperature.                           ║
║        Monitor: add charcoal to kiln every 2 hours to maintain  ║
║        temperature. Keep bellows on LOW — just enough for 900°C.║
║    2d. After 8h: remove box. Cool in box 4+ hours before opening.║
║                                                                  ║
║  PHASE 3 — VERIFY CARBURISATION                                  ║
║    3a. Remove bar. Cut 5mm off one end with file or hacksaw.     ║
║    3b. BEND TEST: heat cut-off piece to 870°C, quench in brine. ║
║        Hold piece in tongs, tap with hammer.                     ║
║        — Breaks easily = >1.0% C (over-carburised; dilute with  ║
║          fresh wrought iron welded on, re-carburise less time). ║
║        — Bends 30° without breaking = 0.5–0.8% C ✓             ║
║        — Barely bends, no fracture = ~0.3–0.5% C (under-done;  ║
║          re-pack and heat 4 more hours).                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  PHASE 4 — NORMALIZE                                             ║
║    4a. Heat bar to 870°C (cherry-red). Air-cool to room temp.   ║
║    4b. Repeat twice. This refines grain structure, removes       ║
║        banding from carburisation, relieves internal stresses.  ║
║                                                                  ║
║  PHASE 5 — HARDEN (QUENCH)                                       ║
║    5a. Heat bar to 790°C (cherry-red — observe IN DARK SHADE).  ║
║        Target: uniform colour throughout — no dark cold spots.  ║
║    5b. Have brine bucket ready. Plunge bar VERTICALLY, edge/tip  ║
║        first. This minimises warping. Hold STILL while quenching.║
║        Do NOT stir or agitate — agitation causes uneven cooling. ║
║    5c. Hold in brine 15 seconds. Remove. Surface at ~200°C still.║
║    5d. Bar is now FULLY MARTENSITIC: HV 650–700. VERY brittle.  ║
║        It WILL snap if dropped or bumped. Handle extremely       ║
║        carefully until tempered.                                 ║
║                                                                  ║
║  PHASE 6 — TEMPER                                                ║
║    6a. Immediately polish one face/spine of bar on 120-grit     ║
║        sandstone until bright metal visible (5 × 2 cm patch).  ║
║    6b. Hold bar above glowing charcoal embers (not in flame).   ║
║        Heat SLOWLY — 5–10 minutes of gentle warming.            ║
║    6c. Watch polished spot in natural light. Colours appear in  ║
║        sequence, moving from hot areas toward the edge:          ║
║                                                                  ║
║        TEMPERING COLOUR TABLE                                    ║
║        ┌──────────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────────┐   ║
║        │ Colour on steel  │ Temperature  │ Recommended use  │   ║
║        ├──────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────┤   ║
║        │ Pale yellow      │ 220°C        │ Engravers, scrapers│  ║
║        │ Straw yellow     │ 240°C        │ Turning tools     │  ║
║        │ Dark straw       │ 250°C        │ Chisels, planes   │  ║
║        │ Brown            │ 260°C        │ Axes, knives      │  ║
║        │ Purple-brown     │ 280°C        │ Swords, springs   │  ║
║        │ Blue-purple      │ 290°C        │ Screwdrivers      │  ║
║        │ Full bright blue │ 300°C        │ Springs, saws     │  ║
║        │ Pale blue        │ 320°C        │ Structural parts  │  ║
║        └──────────────────┴──────────────┴──────────────────┘   ║
║                                                                  ║
║    6d. FOR A CHISEL: quench in water when colour reaches DARK   ║
║        STRAW (250°C) on the polished spot. This locks temper.   ║
║    6e. Bar is now HV 500–550. Tough and hard.                   ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  SKILLS                                                          ║
║    • Fire temperature judgment (900°C for carburising)           ║
║    • Colour recognition for quench temperature (cherry-red)      ║
║    • Temper colour watch: requires GOOD LIGHT and FAST reaction  ║
║      (colours change in seconds at the right temp)              ║
║    TRAINING TIME: 2 weeks (3–4 practice bars before mastery).   ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
