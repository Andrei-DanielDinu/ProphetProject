# R061 — Optical Glass Lens (Plano-Convex, Ground and Polished)

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R061 · OPTICAL LENS — PLANO-CONVEX                      ║
║  End product: 1 optical-quality plano-convex lens, 40mm dia,    ║
║  focal length 100mm (suitable for telescope, microscope use)    ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHAT YOU GET                                                    ║
║    A single glass lens with one flat face and one convex face.  ║
║    When held in sunlight: focuses to a bright point (can burn   ║
║    paper). When paired with another lens: forms a telescope or  ║
║    compound microscope.                                          ║
║                                                                  ║
║    WHY THIS MATTERS:                                             ║
║    — Telescope: sees planet discs, Jovian moons, lunar craters. ║
║      This is a theological and scientific revolution.            ║
║    — Microscope (at 100×): sees cells, bacteria, blood corpuscles.║
║      Establishes germ theory 2,000 years early.                 ║
║    — Magnifying glass: instrument-making, fine engraving.       ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS                                                     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) 150g optical glass blank, 50mm dia × 20mm thick  [→ R060]  ║
║     Use ONLY: decolourised glass (MnO₂ added in R060 step D).  ║
║     Green-tinted glass reduces optical clarity — unacceptable   ║
║     for lenses. Colourless = use 50g MnO₂ per 10 kg batch.     ║
║     Blank must be: bubble-free, inclusion-free, uniform.         ║
║     Cast blank in flat pre-heated granite slab mold.            ║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) Abrasive grits (progressive grades)      [all R000 or TERM.]║
║     ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐║
║     │ Grade   │ Material         │ Source               │ Use  │║
║     ├─────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────────┼──────┤║
║     │ Coarse  │ Coarse quartzite │ River gravel          │ 60-  │║
║     │  grit   │ sand (1 mm)      │ crushed + sieved      │ 100μm│║
║     │ Medium  │ Emery (corundum) │ Eastern Desert        │ 30μm │║
║     │  grit   │ Al₂O₃, Mohs 9   │ schists (27°N 33°E)  │      │║
║     │ Fine    │ Emery, fine      │ Mortar-ground emery   │ 10μm │║
║     │  grit   │                  │                       │      │║
║     │ Polish  │ Tripoli (soft    │ Fine siliceous earth  │  1μm │║
║     │         │ silica powder)   │ or ground opal       │      │║
║     └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘║
║     Emery source (critical): corundum schist, Eastern Desert.   ║
║     Collect dark brown-grey hard rock, crush in stone mortar,  ║
║     wet-sieve to separate grades. Mohs 9 = scratches glass.    ║
║                                                                  ║
║  C) Water (grinding lubricant)              [→ R000-H] ■ TERM. ║
║                                                                  ║
║  D) Polishing lap material                                       ║
║     For final polish: pitch lap (pine pitch + tallow, 1:1 mix) ║
║     heated soft and pressed against curved tool to match lens   ║
║     radius. OR: leather lap with tripoli slurry.                ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • Convex grinding tool: fired clay dome, 50 mm radius         ║
║      (R = 50 mm for 100 mm focal length lens via f = R/(n-1),  ║
║      n ≈ 1.52 for soda-lime glass → f = 50/(0.52) ≈ 96 mm).   ║
║    • Flat lap (ground granite slab, 20 × 20 cm flat surface)    ║
║    • Wooden lens holder (pitch-bond lens to flat wood block)    ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  OPTICS MATHEMATICS (MUST UNDERSTAND BEFORE GRINDING)           ║
║                                                                  ║
║  For a plano-convex lens (one flat, one curved face):           ║
║    Focal length: f = R / (n - 1)                                 ║
║    Where:                                                        ║
║      R = radius of curvature of the convex face (mm)            ║
║      n = refractive index of glass (~1.52 for soda-lime glass)  ║
║      (n-1) = 0.52                                                ║
║                                                                  ║
║    For f = 100 mm: R = 100 × 0.52 = 52 mm                       ║
║    For f = 500 mm (telescope objective): R = 260 mm              ║
║    For f = 25 mm (eyepiece): R = 13 mm (very curved)            ║
║                                                                  ║
║  TELESCOPE GALILEAN (10×):                                       ║
║    Objective: plano-convex, f = 500 mm (R = 260 mm), dia 30 mm ║
║    Eyepiece: plano-concave, f = -50 mm (concave ground)         ║
║    Magnification = f_obj / |f_eye| = 500/50 = 10×              ║
║    Tube length = f_obj - |f_eye| = 500 - 50 = 450 mm           ║
║    (Galilean: virtual image, upright, narrow field of view)     ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — PREPARE BLANK                                          ║
║    1a. Cast glass blank (R060) in flat granite mold. Anneal.    ║
║    1b. The blank is 50 mm dia × 20 mm thick flat disc.           ║
║    1c. Pitch-bond blank to flat wooden holder: heat pine pitch,  ║
║        pour thin layer, press blank in, hold until cool.         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — GENERATE CONVEX CURVE                                  ║
║    2a. The grinding tool is a clay dome with radius of curvature ║
║        = 52 mm (the R we calculated). Make: throw on flat stone  ║
║        a small dome of clay, let harden, fire at 900°C.         ║
║    2b. Place dome (convex face up) on firm surface.             ║
║    2c. Apply water + coarse grit (1 mm emery) on dome surface.  ║
║    2d. Press glass blank (flat face against dome) with steady    ║
║        downward pressure. Stroke figure-8 pattern across dome   ║
║        surface. 100 strokes. This grinds the glass convex.      ║
║    2e. Check progress: wipe glass, hold over white paper in sun. ║
║        Focus point emerging? measure focus distance. Adjust.     ║
║    2f. This takes 1–3 hours per lens for coarse shaping.        ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — FINE GRIND (progressive abrasives)                     ║
║    3a. After coarse (1 mm): switch to 30μm emery + water.       ║
║        Figure-8 strokes, 200 strokes. Inspect for scratch marks ║
║        from coarse grit (look in direct sun: should see uniform ║
║        fine haze, no coarse scratches).                          ║
║    3b. Switch to 10μm emery. 300 strokes.                        ║
║    3c. Surface now appears grey-matte. No deep scratches.       ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 4 — POLISH                                                 ║
║    4a. Make pitch lap: melt pine pitch (40g) + tallow (10g).    ║
║        Pour onto dome surface. Cool 10 min to soft-but-not-flow. ║
║        Press glass lens into pitch — it conforms to lens curve. ║
║        Peel off: pitch retains convex curve matching lens.      ║
║    4b. Apply tripoli slurry (tripoli powder + water, cream       ║
║        consistency) to pitch lap.                                ║
║    4c. Polish: press lens flat on pitch lap. Figure-8 strokes,  ║
║        light downward pressure. 400+ strokes.                    ║
║    4d. After 30 min: check in sunlight. Surface should show     ║
║        near-mirror reflection. Focus point sharp on paper?       ║
║    4e. If needed: continue polishing up to 2 hours.             ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 5 — FLAT FACE POLISH                                       ║
║    5a. Release from holder (heat gently — pitch softens).        ║
║    5b. Reverse lens — now polish the flat face on a flat granite ║
║        lap with same abrasive sequence.                          ║
║    5c. This is easier (flat on flat) — 1 hour total.            ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 6 — TEST AND QUALIFY                                       ║
║    6a. Focal length test: hold lens in sunlight, move white      ║
║        paper until a sharp bright dot forms. Measure distance    ║
║        from lens to paper → this is focal length. Within 10%    ║
║        of target = accept.                                       ║
║    6b. Clarity test: look through lens at a distant object.      ║
║        Sharp edges? No colour fringe? No bubbles in view? → Pass.║
║    6c. Surface test: look at reflection of candle. Even, smooth  ║
║        surface = round reflected image. Irregular = flaws.       ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  SKILLS                                                          ║
║    • Understanding the optics math before starting (non-optional)║
║    • Grinding pressure control (consistent = even curve)         ║
║    • Abrasive progression discipline (do NOT skip grades)        ║
║    • Polishing patience (the difference between good and great   ║
║      optics is 2 more hours of polishing)                       ║
║    TRAINING TIME: 1 month for first acceptable lens.             ║
║    CYCLE TIME: 1–2 days per lens (trained).                     ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
