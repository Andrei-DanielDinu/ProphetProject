# R044 — Hardwood Tongs (Pre-Metal Forge Tongs)

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R044 · HARDWOOD FORGE TONGS                             ║
║  End product: 1 × Pair of Hardwood Clamping Tongs (60 cm)        ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PURPOSE IN THE RECURSIVE CHAIN                                  ║
║    To forge the FIRST iron hammer (R046) and FIRST iron tongs    ║
║    (R045), you must hold a 1,100°C glowing iron bloom or billet  ║
║    on the stone anvil. You cannot touch it with your bare hands. ║
║    Before iron tongs exist, you MUST make hardwood tongs.        ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS                                                     ║
║  A) 2 × Straight seasoned green acacia branches                  ║
║     [→ R000-I] ■ TERMINAL. Length: 75 cm, Diameter: 3.5–4.0 cm.  ║
║     Acacia nilotica has dense, interlocking grain that resists   ║
║     charring and retains structural stiffness when heated.       ║
║  B) 1.5 m Rawhide cordage or wet gut thong [→ R000-K / R006]     ║
║  C) 1 × Dense hardwood pivot dowel (1.5 cm dia × 8 cm long)      ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • Flint knife or ground stone adze [→ R005 / R007]            ║
║    • Flint drill / awl [→ R005]                                  ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — CARVING THE JAWS                                       ║
║    1a. At one end of each branch, carve a flat clamping face     ║
║        10 cm long with cross-hatched friction notches using a    ║
║        flint burin (R005).                                       ║
║    1b. Hollow a slight concave pocket in the jaw faces to grip   ║
║        round iron blooms securely.                               ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — BORING THE PIVOT HOLE                                  ║
║    2a. Measure 15 cm back from the jaw tip on both branches.     ║
║    2b. Bore a 1.5 cm diameter hole through both branches using   ║
║        a flint drill (R005) or bow drill.                        ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — PIVOT ASSEMBLY AND RAWHIDE BINDING                     ║
║    3a. Insert the hardwood dowel (C) through both holes.         ║
║    3b. Soak rawhide thong in water, wrap in a figure-8 around    ║
║        the pivot zone, and tie tight. As it dries, it shrinks    ║
║        drum-tight, creating a firm scissor hinge.                ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 4 — WATERPROOFING & CHAR RESISTANCE                        ║
║    4a. Soak the jaw ends in water for 1 hour before every forging║
║        session. Wet hardwood chars on the surface (pyrolyzes)    ║
║        into insulating carbon without catching fire or losing    ║
║        structural strength during 2-minute contact with bloom.   ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  OPERATING PROTOCOL                                              ║
║    • Grip hot iron for maximum 90 seconds.                       ║
║    • Quench wooden jaws in a bucket of water between heats.      ║
║    • Lifespan: 15–20 forging heats. Keep 3 spare pairs at forge. ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
