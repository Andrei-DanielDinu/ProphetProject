# R047 — Hardened and Tempered Steel Chisel

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R047 · HARDENED & TEMPERED STEEL CHISEL                 ║
║  End product: 1 × Cold Chisel (15 cm long × 2 cm wide × 8 mm)    ║
║  Hardness: Cutting bit HV 520–560 (RC 52–54), Shank HV 250       ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHAT YOU GET                                                    ║
║    A tool steel cold chisel capable of cutting unheated copper,  ║
║    bronze, wrought iron, stone, and carving hardwood.            ║
║    It will not dull, bend, or shatter when struck by an iron     ║
║    hammer. This is the cornerstone of all mechanical engineering.║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS                                                     ║
║  A) 180g Tool steel bar (0.65–0.75% Carbon) [→ R041]             ║
║  B) 1 kg Charcoal [→ R025]                                       ║
║  C) 2 L Quenching brine (10% NaCl in water) [→ R000-H / R000-L]  ║
║  D) Water & fine quartz sand / sandstone slab [→ R000-F]         ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • Forge & pot bellows [→ R021 / R026]                         ║
║    • Granite or iron anvil [→ R002 / R048]                       ║
║    • Iron blacksmith hammer (1.5 kg) [→ R046]                    ║
║    • Iron tongs [→ R045]                                         ║
║    • Sandstone sharpening slab (120 grit and 400 grit)           ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — FORGING THE BLANK                                      ║
║    1a. Grip steel bar (R041) with iron tongs (R045).             ║
║    1b. Heat to bright cherry red (850–900°C) in forge.           ║
║    1c. Rest on anvil. Hammer the striking head into a slight     ║
║        taper (chamfer edges so it won't mushroom under blows).   ║
║    1d. Reheat. Hammer opposite end into a flat cutting taper:    ║
║        width expands to 20 mm, thickness tapers down to 2 mm.    ║
║    1e. DO NOT forge below dark red (700°C) — microcracks form.   ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — NORMALIZING (Stress relief)                            ║
║    2a. Heat entire chisel to 850°C uniformly.                    ║
║    2b. Remove from forge, hang in still air to cool to black.    ║
║    2c. Repeat twice. Realigns crystalline grain structure.       ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — HARDENING (Martensitic Transformation)                 ║
║    3a. Heat only the first 5 cm (cutting end) to 790–810°C       ║
║        (cherry red in shaded light).                             ║
║    3b. Plunge first 3 cm vertically into room-temp brine bucket. ║
║        Hold still for 8 seconds.                                 ║
║    3c. Pull out. The cutting edge is now glass-hard (HV 650+),   ║
║        while the shank behind it remains hot red (500–600°C).    ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 4 — DIFFERENTIAL TEMPERING (Color Run)                     ║
║    4a. IMMEDIATELY polish the hardened flat bevel with a piece   ║
║        of sandstone to reveal bright silver bare metal.          ║
║    4b. Watch the polished metal in natural light:                ║
║        Heat from the hot shank flows forward into the cold bit.  ║
║        Oxide interference colors will crawl toward the edge:     ║
║        Pale yellow (220°C) → Straw (240°C) → Dark Straw (250°C)  ║
║        → Brown (260°C) → Purple (280°C) → Blue (300°C).          ║
║    4c. The instant DARK STRAW / BROWN reaches the cutting edge:  ║
║        Plunge the ENTIRE chisel into water to freeze the temper! ║
║    4d. Result: Edge is tough and hard (HV 540); shank is ductile ║
║        (absorbs hammer strikes without snapping).                ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 5 — SHARPENING AND HONING                                  ║
║    5a. Grind cutting edge on wet sandstone slab to 60° included   ║
║        angle (cold chisel angle for metal cutting) or 25° for    ║
║        woodworking.                                              ║
║    5b. Hone on fine siltstone with water until razor-sharp.      ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROOF TEST: Place cold chisel on a 5 mm thick copper plate.     ║
║  Strike firmly with 1.5 kg hammer. Chisel must sheer cleanly     ║
║  through copper with ZERO denting, rolling, or chipping of edge. ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
