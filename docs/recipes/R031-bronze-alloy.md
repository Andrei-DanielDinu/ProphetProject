# R031 — Bronze (88% Cu : 12% Sn Alloy)

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R031 · TIN BRONZE — ALLOY PRODUCTION                    ║
║  End product: 10 kg bronze ingot, 88Cu:12Sn, HV 160–180        ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHAT YOU GET                                                    ║
║    The canonical Bronze Age alloy. 3× harder than pure copper   ║
║    (HV 180 vs HV 60). Castable at 950°C. Work-hardenable to    ║
║    HV 220+. Corrosion resistant.                                 ║
║    Uses: weapons, cutting tools, bushings, hinges, springs,     ║
║    bowls, instruments. Superior to pure copper for all tools.   ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS (per 10 kg bronze ingot)                           ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) 8.8 kg pure copper metal                    [→ R030]       ║
║     Form: ingot or broken scrap. Must be clean (no clay/slag    ║
║     inclusions). Re-melt and skim if necessary.                  ║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) 1.2 kg cassiterite ore (SnO₂) OR 1.0 kg tin metal          ║
║     [→ R032 for tin smelting from cassiterite]                  ║
║     If using pre-reduced tin metal: 1.0 kg (88+12=100 ✓).      ║
║     If using cassiterite ore (66% Sn): 1.2/0.66 = 1.8 kg ore  ║
║     for 1.2 kg Sn → see R032 for reduction.                    ║
║                                                                  ║
║  C) 2 kg charcoal (fuel + reducing atmosphere)  [→ R025]       ║
║                                                                  ║
║  D) 500g calcined limestone (CaO flux)          [→ R027]       ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHY 88:12 SPECIFICALLY?                                         ║
║    The Cu-Sn phase diagram shows:                                ║
║    • Pure Cu: soft (HV 40–60), melts at 1,083°C.               ║
║    • Cu + 5% Sn: HV ~110. Still ductile.                        ║
║    • Cu + 10% Sn: HV ~160. Good ductility + hardness balance.  ║
║    • Cu + 12% Sn (this recipe): HV ~180. Best hardness before   ║
║      embrittlement begins. Historical optimum.                   ║
║    • Cu + 15% Sn: HV ~180 but brittle. Breaks on impact.        ║
║    • Cu + 20% Sn: HV ~200+ but very brittle. Only use for bells ║
║      (bell metal: 78Cu:22Sn) where hardness > toughness.        ║
║                                                                  ║
║    Rule: DO NOT exceed 14% Sn for tools and weapons.            ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • 1 clay crucible, 3 L capacity              [→ R033]        ║
║    • Forge fire (forced-draft charcoal)         [→ R026]        ║
║    • Clay-coated iron (later) or hardwood stirring rod           ║
║    • Pre-heated stone ingot mold or clay mold   [→ R020]        ║
║    • Iron tongs (later) or hardwood tongs       [→ R041]        ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  CONDITIONS                                                      ║
║    • Melting temperature: copper melts at 1,083°C. Tin at 232°C.║
║      Work at 1,100°C (orange heat) for good fluidity.           ║
║    • Pouring temperature: 950–1,000°C (metal pours like water,  ║
║      fills mold without cold shuts — rough surface = too cool). ║
║    • Atmosphere: reducing (excess charcoal = CO-rich) to prevent ║
║      tin oxide (SnO₂) formation. If Sn oxidises, it appears as  ║
║      grey scum on melt surface.                                  ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — PREPARE CRUCIBLE AND MOLD                              ║
║    1a. Heat crucible to 200°C before loading (prevents thermal  ║
║        shock cracking when hot metal hits cold ceramic).         ║
║    1b. Pre-heat stone mold to 300°C in embers.                  ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — MELT COPPER                                            ║
║    2a. Load crucible: 8.8 kg copper pieces/ingot.               ║
║    2b. Set crucible in bed of charcoal in forge fire.           ║
║    2c. Apply bellows. Bring to 1,100°C.                          ║
║    2d. Time: 15–25 min to full melt (depends on crucible size).  ║
║    2e. When copper is fully liquid (mirror surface): skim any    ║
║        surface dross with clay-coated stick.                     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — ADD TIN                                                ║
║    3a. Reduce bellows briefly to lower temp to ~1,050°C.         ║
║    3b. Add tin (B) in SMALL pieces: 100–200g at a time.          ║
║        Adding all at once causes violent spattering (Sn melts    ║
║        at 232°C — thermal shock when it hits 1,100°C Cu).       ║
║    3c. Stir after each addition with clay-coated rod.           ║
║    3d. Total addition: 1.2 kg tin over ~5 minutes.              ║
║    3e. Stir thoroughly for 2 minutes after last addition.        ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 4 — SKIM AND POUR                                          ║
║    4a. Skim surface with flat stick to remove oxide scum.        ║
║    4b. Pour at 1,000°C. Tilt crucible with tongs. Pour steadily ║
║        in one motion — do not stop mid-pour.                     ║
║    4c. Fill mold to 5 mm above rim (metal contracts ~2% on       ║
║        cooling → will drop to level).                            ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 5 — COOL AND TEST                                          ║
║    5a. Cool in mold 20 min. Remove when surface is black.        ║
║    5b. Test alloy: file a small corner. File removes metal in    ║
║        clean curls (not crumbling) = correct ductility.          ║
║    5c. Hardness test: try to cut with copper chisel edge — if    ║
║        bronze chisel scratches the ingot surface visibly:        ║
║        hardness is HV 160+ = good.                               ║
║    5d. Bend test: heat thin slice to 600°C, quench in water.    ║
║        Bend 90° with pliers — should not crack (if cracks: too  ║
║        much tin — dilute next batch with more copper).           ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  YIELD: 10 kg bronze per batch. Negligible melt losses (<2%).    ║
║  CYCLE TIME: 1 hour (melting + pouring + cooling).              ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  CASTING PRODUCTS FROM BRONZE (downstream of this recipe)       ║
║                                                                  ║
║  Casting uses molds. For each product:                           ║
║    • Pour bronze at 950°C (hotter than optimum: 1,000°C).       ║
║    • Pre-heat stone mold to 300°C always.                        ║
║                                                                  ║
║  Product: Axe head (400g)                                        ║
║    Stone mold carved granite: 15×6×3 cm. Add 2% extra bronze   ║
║    to account for shrinkage (408g poured). After casting:        ║
║    — File edge to 0.5 mm radius bevel.                           ║
║    — Cold-hammer edge 20 blows to work-harden (HV 180→220).    ║
║    — Hafting: bore 3 cm socket in acacia handle, wedge in,      ║
║       lash with rawhide soaked in water (shrinks on drying).    ║
║                                                                  ║
║  Product: Chisel (150g)                                          ║
║    Cast in stone mold. File edge bevel 25°. Cold-hammer.        ║
║    Without iron: best cutting tool available until R040.         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  Product: Saw blade (200g)                                       ║
║    Cast flat, 30 cm × 3 cm × 4 mm. File teeth: 2 mm pitch,     ║
║    30° rake angle, 1 mm depth. 150 teeth per blade.             ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
