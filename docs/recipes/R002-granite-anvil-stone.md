# R002 — Granite Anvil Stone

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R002 · GRANITE ANVIL STONE                               ║
║  End product: 1 × Stationary Working Anvil (30–60 kg, flat top)  ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHY YOU MUST MAKE THIS BEFORE ANY METAL FORGING                 ║
║    When you strike hot iron, bronze, or ore with a 3 kg hammer,  ║
║    Newton's third law dictates that an equal and opposite force  ║
║    must be exerted from below.                                   ║
║    If you hammer on soft ground, clay, or limestone:             ║
║    - Energy is absorbed by the ground (zero work on metal).      ║
║    - Limestone pulverizes to dust instantly under hammer blows.  ║
║    - The hot metal cools before it shapes.                       ║
║    You MUST have a dense, unyielding crystalline anvil stone.    ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS                                                     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) 1 × Massive boulder of Pink or Grey Granite (or Diorite/Gabbro)║
║     [■ TERMINAL — Quarried from river cataracts or wadi cliffs]  ║
║     Mass: 30–60 kg. Minimum dimensions: 35 cm × 25 cm × 25 cm.   ║
║     Compressive strength: >130 MPa. Mohs hardness: 6.5–7.0.      ║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) 50 kg rammed earth, river silt, and gravel for bedding base  ║
║     [→ R000-G/R000-F] ■ TERMINAL                                 ║
║                                                                  ║
║  C) 1 × Heavy hardwood log base (optional but superior):         ║
║     Acacia trunk cross-section, 45 cm diameter × 50 cm high.     ║
║     [→ R000-I] ■ TERMINAL                                        ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • Quartzite maul (R001)                                       ║
║    • Flint chisels / stone spalling wedges (R005)                ║
║    • Flat sandstone grinding slab (R000-F) + water               ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — QUARRYING / SPALLING A FLAT WORKING FACE               ║
║    1a. Locate natural joint or bedding plane on a granite block. ║
║    1b. Thermal spalling method: Lay a thin ridge of glowing dry  ║
║        charcoal along the desired break line. Heat for 20 min.   ║
║    1c. Douse line with cold water. Strike hard with quartzite    ║
║        maul (R001) along line. The granite will shear along      ║
║        the thermal stress plane, creating a flat working face.   ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — DRESSING AND LEVELING THE ANVIL FACE                   ║
║    2a. The face must be flat within 3 mm to forge bars straight. ║
║    2b. Peck down high spots using a sharp 1 kg quartzite cobble. ║
║    2c. Grind flat: Place 2 shovels of sharp quartz sand and      ║
║        water on the face. Rub a flat, heavy sandstone block      ║
║        vigorously back and forth across the face for 1 hour.     ║
║    2d. Result: A smooth, flat working table 25 cm × 20 cm.       ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — SEATING AND BEDDING (Critical for energy return)       ║
║    3a. Dig a pit 40 cm deep in the forge floor.                  ║
║    3b. Option A (Buried in Earth): Pack base with 10 cm gravel,  ║
║        place granite anvil in pit, backfill with clay/gravel,    ║
║        and ram tight with wooden tampers until rock-solid.       ║
║        Top surface should stand 20 cm above floor level.         ║
║    3c. Option B (Log Mount): Carve a 5 cm deep hollow into the   ║
║        top of the upright acacia log. Seat anvil stone into it.  ║
║        Pack joint with lead scrap or pitch-and-sand mastic.      ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  VERIFICATION TEST                                               ║
║    Drop a 1 kg steel/bronze ball or smooth round river stone     ║
║    from 50 cm height onto the center of the anvil face.          ║
║    - It should rebound at least 25 cm (50% restitution).         ║
║    - The stone must not ring dull or wobble.                     ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  CYCLE TIME: 6 hours quarrying & dressing; lifetime: years.      ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
