# R013 — Fire by Friction (Bow Drill Method)

> **Note**: R010 (percussion fire) is the Prophet's preferred method.
> R013 is the **backup method** for when pyrite is unavailable.
> Also useful to teach because it requires only found wood — no special minerals.

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R013 · FIRE BY FRICTION — BOW DRILL                     ║
║  End product: Sustained flame in 3–8 minutes from cold start    ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHAT YOU GET                                                    ║
║    A glowing wood-dust coal, transferable to tinder nest,        ║
║    yielding open flame. Reliability: 80% (trained); 40%         ║
║    (untrained). Harder than percussion method.                   ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS                                                     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) Fireboard (hearth board): 1 piece          [→ R000-I] TERM.║
║     Wood: soft, dry hardwood. Best: acacia, willow, sycamore.   ║
║     NOT pine (too resinous) NOT green wood (too moist).         ║
║     Dimensions: 30 cm × 10 cm × 2 cm flat board.               ║
║     Moisture: bone dry. If in doubt: dry in sun 3 days.         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) Drill spindle: 1 piece                     [→ R000-I] TERM.║
║     Wood: same species as fireboard ideally, OR slightly harder. ║
║     Dimensions: 25–30 cm long, 1.5–2 cm diameter, straight,    ║
║     round (no bumps). Ends: top — blunt-round; bottom — blunt   ║
║     point (45° cone), 1 cm dia.                                 ║
║                                                                  ║
║  C) Bow: 1 piece                               [→ R000-I] TERM.║
║     Curved branch 50–60 cm long, 2 cm dia.                      ║
║     Bow string: 60 cm sinew, leather thong, or plant cordage.   ║
║     Tension: loose enough to wrap 1 turn around spindle and     ║
║     still allow smooth sawing. Test: string wraps spindle snugly.║
║                                                                  ║
║  D) Handhold (top cap): 1 piece                                  ║
║     Hard, smooth wood or stone with a small cup-depression for  ║
║     the top of the spindle. 8×5×3 cm. Use a palm-sized stone    ║
║     with natural depression, or carve cup in hardwood piece.     ║
║     Lubricate cup with: earwax, fresh plant sap, or tallow.     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  E) Dry tinder nest, 10g                       [→ R012]        ║
║                                                                  ║
║  F) Lubrication for handhold (not for fireboard!)                ║
║     1 small amount (~0.5g) tallow, earwax, or green leaf sap.   ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • Flint or copper knife to shape parts   [→ R030 for copper] ║
║    • 1 small flat leaf or bark chip as coal-catcher              ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  CONDITIONS                                                      ║
║    • Fireboard and spindle MUST be dry. This is the #1 failure.  ║
║      Test: press thumbnail into wood. If leaves a mark easily   ║
║      (like pressing into apple flesh) → too soft/wet. Find       ║
║      drier, denser wood.                                         ║
║    • Wind: shelter from wind (blows coal away).                  ║
║    • The pairing matters: use same or similar wood species for   ║
║      board and spindle. Mixed hard+soft works. Both very hard    ║
║      or both very soft usually fails.                            ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS — PREPARATION (one-time setup per fireboard)           ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP P1 — CUT NOTCH IN FIREBOARD                                ║
║    P1a. Place fireboard flat on ground.                          ║
║    P1b. With flint or knife, carve a shallow round depression    ║
║         1 cm from the edge of the board: 2 cm diameter, 3 mm    ║
║         deep. This is the "burn-in" hole.                        ║
║    P1c. First use: drill for 30 seconds until the hole blackens  ║
║         (this "seasons" the hole to match the spindle shape).    ║
║    P1d. Cut a V-notch: from the edge of the board, cut a         ║
║         triangular notch into the seasoned hole. The notch tip   ║
║         should reach the CENTRE of the hole. Width at board edge:║
║         5 mm. This notch collects the hot wood dust (the coal).  ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP P2 — POSITION COAL CATCHER                                 ║
║    P2a. Place a flat bark chip or leaf under the V-notch.        ║
║         This catches the hot dust that becomes your coal.        ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS — DRILLING                                              ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — SETUP POSITION                                         ║
║    1a. Kneel on right knee. Place left foot on fireboard to hold ║
║        it steady. The V-notch and coal-catcher are at your left  ║
║        foot's inner edge.                                        ║
║    1b. Wrap bow string ONE turn around spindle middle.           ║
║    1c. Place spindle bottom point in the seasoned hole.          ║
║    1d. Place handhold on spindle top. Hold handhold firmly       ║
║        against your left shin for stability — wrist locked.      ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — DRILL                                                  ║
║    2a. Saw bow back and forth with right hand. FULL strokes —    ║
║        all the way back, all the way forward. 60–80 strokes/min. ║
║    2b. Apply steady DOWNWARD pressure through the handhold.      ║
║        More pressure = more friction = more heat. But too much   ║
║        and spindle will pop out of hole.                         ║
║    2c. After 20–30 strokes: smoke appears. Keep going.           ║
║    2d. After 60–90 strokes: thick white smoke. Rate and pressure ║
║        should be MAXIMUM now. Count to 10 at full speed.         ║
║    2e. Stop. CAREFULLY lift spindle and board straight up        ║
║        WITHOUT disturbing the coal-catcher.                      ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — CHECK COAL                                             ║
║    3a. Look at the coal-catcher. There should be a small pile of ║
║        dark powder, with one glowing red-orange spot = your coal.║
║    3b. If no glow: resume drilling immediately, 20 more strokes. ║
║    3c. If glow: gently tap edge of coal-catcher to consolidate  ║
║        dust around the glowing spot.                             ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 4 — TRANSFER AND IGNITE                                    ║
║    4a. Same as R010 Steps 5–6:                                   ║
║        Tip coal into centre of tinder nest. Fold. Blow steadily. ║
║        Flame in 15–30 seconds.                                   ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  SKILLS                                                          ║
║    • Finding and identifying dry suitable wood (most critical).  ║
║    • Bow technique: consistent full strokes without stopping.    ║
║    • Pressure judgment: too little → no coal; too much → pops.  ║
║                                                                  ║
║    TRAINING TIME: 4–6 hours to first success; 10 hours for       ║
║    consistent 80% reliability. Hardest primitive skill.          ║
║    CYCLE TIME (trained): 3–8 minutes depending on wood quality.  ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROPHET'S NOTE                                                  ║
║    Teach this but do not rely on it. Percussion fire (R010) with ║
║    pyrite + flint is 10× faster and more reliable. The bow drill ║
║    is for when pyrite supplies are disrupted or in distant        ║
║    expeditions where carrying a fire kit is not possible.        ║
║    However: the bow drill teaches mechanical understanding of    ║
║    friction, heat generation, and material selection — teach it  ║
║    in the school's physics curriculum.                           ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
