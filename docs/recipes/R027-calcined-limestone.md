# R027 — Calcined Limestone (Quicklime / CaO)

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R027 · CALCINED LIMESTONE (QUICKLIME)                   ║
║  End product: 50 kg quicklime (CaO), flux-grade                 ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHAT YOU GET                                                    ║
║    Calcium oxide (CaO). White to grey chunks. Highly caustic.   ║
║    Uses in this project:                                         ║
║    1. Flux in copper smelting (binds silica gangue to slag)      ║
║    2. Causticizing natron to make NaOH (for soap [→ R050])      ║
║    3. Lime plaster (mixed with water and sand → mortar)          ║
║    4. Disinfectant (lime wash on walls, floors — kills bacteria) ║
║    5. Leather processing (lime pit for de-hairing hides)         ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS (for 50 kg CaO output)                             ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) 90 kg limestone (CaCO₃)              [→ R000-J] ■ TERM.   ║
║     Math: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂              MW: 100 → 56 + 44     ║
║     56/100 = 56% yield by weight → 90 kg × 56% = ~50 kg CaO.  ║
║     Preferred: white, pure limestone (grey may have clay/Mg).   ║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) 100 kg charcoal (fuel)               [→ R025]              ║
║     Calcination temperature: 900°C sustained 2 hours.           ║
║     Requires kiln temperature — open fire alone is insufficient. ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • Updraft mudbrick kiln (or separate lime kiln)   [→ R021]  ║
║    • Pot bellows                                      [→ R026]  ║
║    • Metal or wooden shovel / broad wooden paddle               ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  CONDITIONS                                                      ║
║    • Temperature: minimum 900°C sustained for 2 hours.          ║
║      Below 900°C: incomplete calcination (CaCO₃ remains in core)║
║      This is acceptable as mixed product but reduces flux effect.║
║    • Atmosphere: oxidising (normal air) — no need for reducing   ║
║      atmosphere unlike metal smelting.                           ║
║    • After calcination: quicklime is VERY reactive. Keep dry.   ║
║      Contact with water: vigorous exothermic reaction (160°C    ║
║      local hotspot). Store in sealed clay pots.                  ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — PREPARE LIMESTONE                                      ║
║    1a. Break limestone blocks to 5–10 cm pieces (not smaller —  ║
║        small fragments may blow out in draft).                   ║
║    1b. No other preparation needed.                              ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — LOAD AND FIRE                                          ║
║    2a. Load kiln: alternate charcoal layer (5 cm) / limestone   ║
║        layer (15 cm). Fill to 70 cm height.                      ║
║    2b. Ignite. Apply bellows.                                    ║
║    2c. Reach 900°C (orange-red in kiln chamber). Hold 2 hours.  ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — EXTRACT                                                ║
║    3a. Cool 4 hours before handling.                             ║
║    3b. Remove: white/grey chalky pieces (successfully calcined), ║
║        any unchanged grey limestone (failed — returns to mix),   ║
║        ash from charcoal (discard or use as alkali source).      ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 4 — TEST                                                   ║
║    4a. Drop 1 small piece (5g) into 50 mL water. If it:         ║
║        — Fizzes, heats up, disintegrates over 10 min = CaO ✓    ║
║        — Does nothing = unreacted CaCO₃ (re-fire)               ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 5 — STORE                                                  ║
║    5a. Place immediately in sealed clay pots.                    ║
║    5b. Even humid air will begin slaking (CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂, ║
║        releasing heat). Slaked lime is still useful but weaker  ║
║        as flux — use fresh quicklime for smelting flux.          ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  SAFETY                                                          ║
║    ⚠ Quicklime dust is caustic — burns eyes and mucous membranes.║
║    ⚠ Adding water creates vigorous HEAT — do not add to wet    ║
║      areas. Use in shade — if accidentally gets wet, stand clear.║
║    ⚠ Store away from water sources and wet clay areas.          ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  SKILLS: None beyond fire management. First batch any smith.    ║
║  CYCLE TIME: 2h fire + 4h cool = half-day per 50 kg batch.     ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
