# R040 — Bloomery Iron (Wrought Iron from Hematite)

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R040 · BLOOMERY IRON — WROUGHT IRON BAR                 ║
║  End product: 2.5 kg wrought iron bar, <0.1% C, malleable      ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHAT YOU GET                                                    ║
║    Pure iron (wrought iron): soft, ductile, weldable, rust-prone.║
║    Density: 7.6 g/cm³. Hardness: HV 80–120 (annealed).         ║
║    Use: convert to steel (R041), make nails, wire, structural   ║
║    bars, eventually machine components.                          ║
║    This is the MOST IMPORTANT step in the metallurgical chain — ║
║    iron tools are 3–10× more efficient than bronze for cutting, ║
║    mining, and construction.                                     ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS (per bloom run, → 2.5 kg wrought iron)             ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) 10 kg pre-roasted hematite ore (≥50% Fe)    [→ R042]       ║
║     Math: 10 kg × 60% Fe × 50% extraction = ~3 kg Fe target.  ║
║     Minus hammer-out losses (15%) = ~2.5 kg wrought iron bar.  ║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) 15 kg charcoal                              [→ R025]        ║
║     Ratio: 1.5 kg charcoal per 1 kg ore (by weight).            ║
║     Charcoal serves TWO roles:                                   ║
║     1. FUEL: burns to generate 1,200–1,300°C with bellows air.  ║
║     2. REDUCTANT: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ (CO from charcoal). ║
║                                                                  ║
║  C) 500g calcined limestone (CaO flux)          [→ R027]        ║
║     Binds silica gangue → CaSiO₃ slag (liquid, flows out).      ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • Bloomery furnace (clay shaft)               [→ R043]       ║
║    • 2 pot bellows (continuous draft)            [→ R026]       ║
║    • 3 kg quartzite cobble hammer (for bloom)    [■ TERMINAL]   ║
║    • Flat granite anvil stone, 30+ kg             [■ TERMINAL]  ║
║    • Hardwood tongs, 60 cm long                  [→ R044]       ║
║    • Iron tongs (later, once iron available)     [→ R041]       ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  CONDITIONS                                                      ║
║    • Peak furnace temperature: 1,200–1,300°C (iron does NOT melt  ║
║      — melting point 1,538°C. Iron SINTERS in solid state into  ║
║      a spongy "bloom" at 1,200°C with CO atmosphere).            ║
║    • Bellows: continuous. 2 operators, 15-min rotating shifts.  ║
║    • Run time: 3–4 hours continuous from charge to bloom.       ║
║    • CRITICAL: atmosphere is REDUCING (CO-rich). If oxidising   ║
║      (air excess), iron re-oxidises back to ore. Watch for:     ║
║      bright orange-white tuyere zone (correct) vs. short dim    ║
║      red zone (insufficient heat = insufficient reduction).      ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  FURNACE SETUP (from R043 — bloomery-specific configuration)    ║
║                                                                  ║
║    ════════════════  ← open top (no flue cap needed)             ║
║    │                │                                            ║
║    │  CHARGE ZONE   │  ← ore + charcoal charge (90 cm tall)     ║
║    │                │                                            ║
║    │────────────────│  ← BLOOM ZONE: 1,200°C here               ║
║    │   ► TUYERE     │  ← 1 tuyere, 4 cm ID, 20° downward,      ║
║    │                │    tip 8 cm from inner wall                ║
║    │  SLAG/BLOOM    │  ← iron sinters here; slag pools liquid    ║
║    ╘════╤═══════════╛                                            ║
║         │ slag tap hole (3 cm dia)                               ║
║         │ ← tap every 60 min                                     ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — PREPARE ORE (previous day or morning)                  ║
║    1a. Crush hematite to 1–3 cm pieces.                          ║
║    1b. ROAST: spread on flat stone, heat over open fire 1 hour  ║
║        at 600°C. Red-brown → black (Fe₂O₃·H₂O → Fe₂O₃). Loses ║
║        ~15% mass. This drives out moisture and pre-cracks ore.  ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — FURNACE PREPARATION                                    ║
║    2a. Inspect furnace. Tap hole sealed with clay plug?          ║
║    2b. Preheat: light small fire in bottom (20 min at 300°C) to ║
║        drive moisture from furnace walls.                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — CHARGE LOADING                                         ║
║    Load in alternating layers from the bottom. The charge        ║
║    descends as it reacts — keep topping up throughout the run.  ║
║                                                                  ║
║    Initial charge sequence:                                      ║
║    Layer 1: 3 kg charcoal (20 cm depth)                          ║
║    Layer 2: 2 kg roasted ore + 100g CaO (10 cm)                 ║
║    Layer 3: 3 kg charcoal (20 cm)                                ║
║    Layer 4: 2 kg ore + 100g CaO (10 cm)                         ║
║    Layer 5: 3 kg charcoal (20 cm)                                ║
║    Layer 6: 2 kg ore + 100g CaO (10 cm)                         ║
║    Layer 7: 3 kg charcoal (top, 20 cm)                           ║
║    — Total initial: 15 kg charcoal, 6 kg ore (add rest as run   ║
║      progresses, charge sinks in).                               ║
║    — Total run: 15 kg charcoal, 10 kg ore, 500g CaO.           ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 4 — IGNITE AND BEGIN BELLOWS                               ║
║    4a. Light base through tap hole or stoke access.              ║
║    4b. Begin bellows immediately. 2 operators alternating.       ║
║    4c. Temperature progression:                                  ║
║        30 min: dark red (~650°C) — ore begins reducing           ║
║        60 min: bright cherry (~800°C) — CO reducing zone grows  ║
║        90 min: orange-red (~1,000°C) — sponge iron forming      ║
║        120 min: yellow-orange (~1,100°C) — bloom consolidating  ║
║        150–180 min: white-orange (~1,200–1,300°C) → BLOOM       ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 5 — TAP SLAG (every 60 min)                                ║
║    5a. Liquid slag (black-grey, less dense than iron) pools at  ║
║        base. Tap every hour to prevent flooding the bloom zone. ║
║    5b. Punch tap hole plug, let slag run out (black liquid,     ║
║        quenches to dark glass-like solid). Re-plug after 30 sec.║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 6 — EXTRACT THE BLOOM                                      ║
║    6a. After 3–4 hours: glow at tuyere is white-orange. Charge  ║
║        is consumed (level drops to 20 cm above base). Stop       ║
║        adding new charge. Run bellows 10 more minutes.           ║
║    6b. Stop bellows. IMMEDIATELY break open furnace base (remove ║
║        2 base bricks or punch tap hole large). Working fast      ║
║        (bloom cools in 2–3 min from 1,200° to unworkable 800°C).║
║    6c. Extract bloom with long tongs or hardwood poles.          ║
║        Bloom appears: spongy grey-orange mass, 3–5 kg, porous.  ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 7 — HAMMER THE BLOOM (critical — cannot wait)             ║
║    7a. Bloom at 1,100°C: place on stone anvil. 3 people: one    ║
║        holds bloom with tongs, two hammer.                       ║
║    7b. Strike HARD: 3 kg quartzite hammer, 2 blows/sec.         ║
║        Slag squirts out as liquid between strikes (bright orange ║
║        blobs — STAND BACK, wear leather on face/chest).          ║
║    7c. Work all faces. Turn bloom 90° between strike sequences. ║
║    7d. When surface turns dull red (~800°C): return bloom to    ║
║        charcoal forge fire. Add bellows. 5 min to orange.        ║
║    7e. Hammer again. Repeat 6–10 reheats total.                 ║
║    7f. After 6–10 reheats: bloom has consolidated. Iron is now  ║
║        wrought iron (slag ~1–2% by weight, filament-like).       ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 8 — SHAPE INTO BAR                                         ║
║    8a. While still hot (orange-red): hammer into desired bar    ║
║        cross-section. E.g.: 20 mm × 20 mm × 25 cm long.        ║
║    8b. Straighten while hot.                                     ║
║    8c. Final bloom weight: ~2.5 kg from 10 kg ore.              ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  YIELD AND SCALING                                               ║
║    Per run: 10 kg ore → 2.5 kg wrought iron. 4-hour active run. ║
║    With 2 furnaces, 2 runs/day: 10 kg wrought iron/day.         ║
║    Year 1 target: 300 kg wrought iron (120 days × 2.5 kg/day).  ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  SKILLS                                                          ║
║    • Bloom extraction timing: most critical — cannot wait.       ║
║    • Hammer technique: even blows, constant rotation.            ║
║    • PHYSICAL ENDURANCE: hammering hot metal is hard work.       ║
║      3-person hammer team recommended. Rotate hourly.            ║
║    TRAINING TIME: 1 month to reliable bloom production.          ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  SAFETY                                                          ║
║    ⚠ Slag spray during hammering: 1,100°C liquid slag droplets.  ║
║      All nearby workers must wear thick leather aprons/face      ║
║      protection. Minimum 3 m clearance for bystanders.          ║
║    ⚠ Hot bloom collapses if dropped — keep tongs locked.         ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
