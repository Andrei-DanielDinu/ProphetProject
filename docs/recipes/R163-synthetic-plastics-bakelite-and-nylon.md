# R163 — Synthetic Plastics: Bakelite and Nylon 6,6

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R163 · SYNTHETIC POLYMERS (THERMOSET & THERMOPLASTIC)    ║
║  Products: 1. Bakelite Phenol-Formaldehyde Resin (Thermoset)     ║
║            2. Nylon 6,6 Polyamide Fiber (Thermoplastic)          ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHY SYNTHETICS LIBERATE PRODUCTION FROM NATURE                  ║
║    Before synthetic chemistry: insulation was mica or shellac,   ║
║    textiles were hand-harvested cotton and silk, gears were cast ║
║    bronze. Polymer chemistry cross-links petroleum and coal      ║
║    molecules into designer materials that never corrode, insulate║
║    10,000 Volts, and outlast natural fibers by decades!          ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PART 1: BAKELITE THERMOSETTING RESIN (Leo Baekeland, 1907)      ║
║                                                                  ║
║  CHEMICAL CONDENSATION:                                          ║
║    Phenol (C₆H₅OH) + Formaldehyde (HCHO) ──► Novolac / Resol     ║
║    Under heat and pressure, methylene (-CH₂-) bridges cross-link ║
║    aromatic rings into a rigid 3D insoluble macromolecular cage! ║
║                                                                  ║
║  INGREDIENTS:                                                    ║
║  A) 500g Phenol (Carbolic acid crystals) [→ Coal tar distillation║
║     or benzene sulfonation with H₂SO₄ R053 + NaOH R052]          ║
║  B) 400 mL Formalin solution (37% Formaldehyde in water)         ║
║     [→ Made by catalytic air oxidation of methanol over hot      ║
║     copper gauze catalyst at 500°C: CH₃OH + ½ O₂ ──► HCHO + H₂O] ║
║  C) 20 mL Ammonia water (NH₄OH, catalyst) [→ R055]               ║
║  D) 400g Wood flour / fine cellulose sawdust (R000-I, filler)    ║
║                                                                  ║
║  PROCESS:                                                        ║
║  1. Mix phenol, formalin, and ammonia in steam-heated kettle.    ║
║  2. Heat to 100°C for 2 hours: viscous golden resin forms.       ║
║  3. Dehydrate resin under vacuum: cools into brittle solid.       ║
║  4. Grind to powder; blend with 50% wood flour filler.           ║
║  5. Compression molding: Place powder into steel die at 170°C,   ║
║     clamp under 200 bar hydraulic pressure for 3 minutes:        ║
║     Molecules cross-link permanently: pops out as an infusible,  ║
║     glass-hard electrical insulator (switchboard panels, plugs,  ║
║     radio casings, distributor caps)!                            ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PART 2: NYLON 6,6 CONDENSATION POLYMER (Wallace Carothers, 1935)║
║                                                                  ║
║  CHEMICAL CONDENSATION:                                          ║
║    n H₂N-(CH₂)₆-NH₂  +  n HOOC-(CH₂)₄-COOH  ──►                  ║
║    [-NH-(CH₂)₆-NH-CO-(CH₂)₄-CO-]n  +  2n H₂O                    ║
║    (Hexamethylenediamine + Adipic Acid ──► Nylon Salt ──► Polymer)║
║                                                                  ║
║  PROCESS:                                                        ║
║  1. Dissolve equimolar hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid in   ║
║     ethanol: pure white crystals of "Nylon Salt" precipitate.    ║
║  2. Charge salt into stainless steel autoclave; heat to 280°C at ║
║     18 bar pressure for 3 hours while venting steam:             ║
║     Condensation polymerizes into high-molecular-weight nylon melt║
║  3. Melt Spinning: Pump molten polymer at 290°C through a spinneret║
║     die containing 64 laser-drilled 0.2 mm holes: filaments emerge║
║     and solidify in cold air cross-blast.                         ║
║  4. Cold Drawing: Pull fiber filaments through godet rolls at    ║
║     400% stretch: polymer molecules align parallel along axis,   ║
║     increasing tensile strength to 600 MPa (stronger than steel  ║
║     by weight!). Woven into parachute cloth, cords, and gears.   ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
