# R022 — Mudbrick (Sun-Dried)

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R022 · MUDBRICK (SUN-DRIED)                             ║
║  End product: 100 mudbricks, 36×18×9 cm each, mass ~12 kg each  ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHAT YOU GET                                                    ║
║    Sun-dried mudbricks for general construction (walls, kilns,   ║
║    ovens, irrigation banks). NOT fired — for fired brick see     ║
║    R021 (kiln operation). Sun-dried is adequate for most uses    ║
║    not exposed to direct sustained fire or water immersion.      ║
║    Compressive strength: 1–3 MPa (adequate for 2-storey walls). ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS (per 100 bricks)                                    ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) 1,200 kg raw Nile clay / alluvial silt    [→ R000-G] TERM. ║
║     The standard Nile floodplain silt is ideal: naturally mixed  ║
║     clay + silt + fine sand. Colour: dark grey-brown when wet.   ║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) 12 kg chopped dry straw (binder)         [→ R000-I] TERM.  ║
║     Wheat straw or barley straw. Length: 3–8 cm pieces.         ║
║     Straw adds tensile strength. Without it: bricks crack on    ║
║     drying. Ratio: 1 kg straw per 100 kg clay.                   ║
║                                                                  ║
║  C) 60 L water (for mixing)                  [→ R000-H] TERM.  ║
║                                                                  ║
║  D) 2 kg fine sand (optional — only if clay too sticky)         ║
║     River sand, 0.5 mm grain. Reduces shrinkage cracking.       ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • Wooden brick mould (3-compartment)       [→ R024]          ║
║      Internal size: 36×18×9 cm per cell.                        ║
║    • Mixing pit (excavated 1 m × 2 m × 0.5 m)  — dig in earth  ║
║    • Wooden paddle for mixing (60 cm handle)   [→ R000-I] TERM.║
║    • Flat ground for drying                                      ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  CONDITIONS                                                      ║
║    • Dry season production (November–April): optimal.            ║
║    • Wet season: bricks take 2× longer to dry; protect from rain.║
║    • Drying temperature: 30–45°C (direct sun in Egypt — perfect).║
║    • Drying time: 3–5 days each side. Total: 6–10 days.         ║
║    • Humidity: low humidity = faster drying. High = longer.      ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — PREPARE CLAY MIX (per 100 bricks, 1,200 kg clay)      ║
║    1a. Excavate clay from bank or dig pit near Nile (after flood ║
║        recession — October–November — clay freshly exposed).     ║
║    1b. Break up clay clods in mixing pit. Remove pebbles >2 cm. ║
║    1c. Add water gradually (60 L over 30 min). Mix by foot       ║
║        (traditional method: 2 workers walk in circles in pit).  ║
║    1d. Mix for 20 min until uniform, smooth, no dry lumps.       ║
║    1e. Add chopped straw (12 kg). Mix further 10 min.           ║
║    1f. Test consistency: grab handful, squeeze → it holds shape, ║
║        does not ooze through fingers = correct. Adjust with water ║
║        or more clay if needed.                                   ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — MOULD BRICKS                                           ║
║    2a. Wet the wooden mould (prevents sticking).                 ║
║    2b. Throw a handful of clay into each mould cell with force   ║
║        (removes air pockets). Fill all cells proud.              ║
║    2c. Strike off excess with wooden straight-edge, flush.       ║
║    2d. Lift mould carefully straight up. Bricks sit on flat      ║
║        ground. If a brick tears or slumps: too wet — wait 30 min,║
║        rework clay.                                              ║
║    2e. Skilled team rate: 500 bricks per 2-person team per day. ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — DRY                                                    ║
║    3a. Leave bricks in direct sun on flat ground, 3 days.       ║
║    3b. Turn bricks on their side (edge) for 2 more days.        ║
║    3c. Stand bricks upright (on end) for 2 more days if needed. ║
║    3d. Test: tap with knuckle → solid ring (not hollow dull).   ║
║        Try to scratch with thumbnail → surface resists scratching.║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 4 — STACK                                                  ║
║    4a. Stack in bonded piles (alternating course directions) of  ║
║        up to 20 courses. No mortar needed for stacking/storage. ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  SKILLS: None beyond following the recipe. Quickest craft.      ║
║  CYCLE TIME: 1 day production + 7 days drying.                  ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PRODUCTION SCALING                                              ║
║    • 10-person team: 2,500 bricks per day.                      ║
║    • To build 1 updraft kiln (R021): 2,000 bricks = 0.8 day.   ║
║    • To build a 20-room workshop complex: ~50,000 bricks = 20   ║
║      day-equivalents for a 10-person team.                       ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
