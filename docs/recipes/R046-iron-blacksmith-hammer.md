# R046 — Iron Blacksmith Hammer (Cross-Peen Hand Hammer)

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R046 · FORGED IRON BLACKSMITH HAMMER (1.5 kg)           ║
║  End product: 1 × Cross-Peen Hand Hammer (Flat Face + Wedge Peen)║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHY THIS TOOL IS THE HEART OF CIVILIZATION                      ║
║    You used a stone maul (R001) to make your first iron bloom.   ║
║    Now you must forge that iron into an IRON HAMMER.             ║
║    Stone crushes under shock; iron rebounds, focuses kinetic     ║
║    energy, and allows precise control to draw out metal bars,    ║
║    forge tools, and manufacture machinery.                       ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS                                                     ║
║  A) 1.8 kg Wrought iron bloom/billet [→ R040]                    ║
║  B) 100g High-carbon steel scrap or bone-char pack [→ R041/R042] ║
║     (For forge-welding or carburizing the striking face).        ║
║  C) 1 × Seasoned acacia or ash hammer handle (38 cm long)        ║
║     [→ R000-I] ■ TERMINAL. Oval cross-section (3.0 × 2.0 cm).    ║
║  D) 1 × Hardwood or iron wedge (2.5 cm × 1.5 cm × 3 mm)          ║
║  E) 4 kg Charcoal [→ R025]                                       ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • Forge & pot bellows [→ R021 / R026]                         ║
║    • Granite anvil stone [→ R002]                                ║
║    • Heavy quartzite maul [→ R001]                               ║
║    • Hardwood tongs [→ R044] (or first iron tongs R045)          ║
║    • Tapered iron drift / eye-punch (2.5 cm × 1.5 cm oval)       ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — CONSOLIDATING THE HAMMER HEAD BILLET                   ║
║    1a. Heat 1.8 kg wrought iron block to bright yellow (1,150°C).║
║    1b. Strike with quartzite maul (R001) on stone anvil (R002),  ║
║        hammering into a square block: 12 cm long × 4.5 × 4.5 cm. ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — PUNCHING AND DRIFTING THE EYE (Handle Hole)            ║
║    2a. Mark center of block (6 cm from end).                     ║
║    2b. Heat block to near-white heat (1,200°C).                  ║
║    2c. Place tapered oval punch on center mark. Drive punch into ║
║        the hot iron halfway through.                             ║
║    2d. Flip block 180°. Drive punch from opposite side until the ║
║        slug pops out cleanly.                                    ║
║    2e. Insert tapered steel drift. Drive drift through eye to    ║
║        expand and form an hourglass-shaped eye (wider at top and ║
║        bottom, narrow at waist — locks the wooden handle wedge). ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — SHAPING THE FACE AND CROSS-PEEN                        ║
║    3a. Hammer front face square (3.5 × 3.5 cm) with chamfered    ║
║        corners to prevent chipping.                              ║
║    3b. Hammer back face into a tapered cross-peen wedge (used    ║
║        for drawing out hot iron longitudinally).                 ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 4 — CARBURIZING AND HARDENING THE STRIKING FACE            ║
║    4a. Pack striking face in bone-char paste (R042).             ║
║    4b. Heat in forge to 900°C for 2 hours (case hardening).      ║
║    4c. Heat whole head to cherry red (800°C). Dip ONLY the front ║
║        face 2 cm into brine (R000-L) for 5 seconds.              ║
║    4d. Polish face with sandstone. Watch residual heat from core ║
║        temper the face to purple/blue (280–300°C). Full quench.  ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 5 — HAFTING THE HANDLE                                     ║
║    5a. Shave acacia handle to fit eye snugly.                    ║
║    5b. Saw 2 cm slit in top of handle. Drive handle into eye.    ║
║    5c. Drive iron/hardwood wedge (D) into slit, spreading wood   ║
║        into the hourglass eye cavity. Cut flush.                 ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TEST: Strike granite anvil full-power 10 times. Zero loosening. ║
║  Output: The tool that will forge all future steel tools.        ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
