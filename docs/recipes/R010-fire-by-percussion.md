# R010 — Fire by Percussion (Pyrite + Flint Method)

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R010 · FIRE BY PERCUSSION                               ║
║  End product: Sustained flame from cold start in < 60 seconds   ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHAT YOU GET                                                    ║
║    A single sustained flame capable of igniting kindling,        ║
║    lighting a torch, or starting a forge fire.                   ║
║    Reliability: >95% first-attempt success (trained operator).   ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS                                                     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) 1 piece iron pyrite, fist-sized       [→ R000-B]  ■ TERM.  ║
║     Size: 8×6×4 cm minimum. Mass: ~200g.                        ║
║     Condition: sharp edge on at least one face.                  ║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) 1 piece flint or chert               [→ R000-A]  ■ TERM.  ║
║     Size: palm-sized, 100–200g. Must have a fresh sharp edge     ║
║     (not weather-rounded). Mohs 7 — MUST be harder than pyrite. ║
║                                                                  ║
║  C) 1 piece char cloth, 4×4 cm           [→ R011]              ║
║     Condition: fully charred (jet black, no brown patches).      ║
║     Zero moisture — stored in a sealed clay pot or dry pouch.    ║
║                                                                  ║
║  D) 1 handful dry tinder, ~10g           [→ R012]              ║
║     Options: dry grass, cattail fluff, dry cedar bark shreds.    ║
║     Condition: bone dry (crispy, not limp). Zero moisture.       ║
║                                                                  ║
║  E) 2–3 sticks of kindling, 1 cm dia     [→ R000-I]  ■ TERM.  ║
║     Dry acacia or tamarisk. Bone dry. 15 cm long.               ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • Your own two hands                                          ║
║    • Optional: leather pad or folded hide for holding pyrite     ║
║      (protects hand, improves grip)                              ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  CONDITIONS                                                      ║
║    • Wind: shelter the char cloth from wind until it glows       ║
║    • Moisture: char cloth MUST be dry. If humid (>70% RH),      ║
║      warm char cloth near existing fire before attempting.       ║
║    • Temperature: works in any temperature. Cold slows but does  ║
║      not prevent ignition. Snow/wet conditions: protect materials║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — PREPARE TINDER NEST (do this first, 2 min)            ║
║    1a. Take your handful of dry tinder (D).                      ║
║    1b. Work it between both palms in a circular motion for       ║
║        30 seconds until fibres loosen.                           ║
║    1c. Shape into a loose bird's-nest: 10 cm across, dense at   ║
║        outside, hollow at centre (5 cm hollow). Set aside.       ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — POSITION CHAR CLOTH                                    ║
║    2a. Hold pyrite (A) in dominant hand, sharp edge up.          ║
║    2b. Drape char cloth (C) over the sharp top edge of pyrite.   ║
║        Cloth should hang 1 cm over the strike face.              ║
║    2c. Hold both together — cloth stays in place by gentle       ║
║        thumb pressure.                                           ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — STRIKE                                                 ║
║    3a. Hold flint (B) in other hand, sharp edge downward.        ║
║    3b. Bring flint DOWN against the pyrite in a sharp            ║
║        DOWNWARD SCRAPING motion — not a tap, but a strike        ║
║        where the flint edge drags 2–3 cm along the pyrite face.  ║
║    3c. Strike speed: fast (0.5 m/s wrist snap). Slow strikes     ║
║        produce no sparks.                                        ║
║    3d. Strike angle: 30–45° off vertical.                        ║
║    3e. Target: direct sparks ONTO the char cloth.                ║
║    3f. Each strike should produce 3–8 visible white-yellow       ║
║        sparks. Sparks are ~700–900°C.                            ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 4 — CATCH COAL                                             ║
║    4a. After 1–5 strikes, char cloth will show a glowing red-    ║
║        orange spot (a "coal"). This coal is ~600°C.              ║
║    4b. Immediately fold char cloth around the coal — do not      ║
║        blow yet. The fold protects the coal and smothers it      ║
║        enough to prevent it burning too fast.                    ║
║    4c. Coal will spread slowly through char cloth (30 seconds).  ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 5 — TRANSFER TO TINDER NEST                                ║
║    5a. Place folded, glowing char cloth into the hollow centre   ║
║        of your tinder nest.                                      ║
║    5b. Fold the nest loosely around it. Hold nest in both hands. ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 6 — BLOW TO FLAME                                          ║
║    6a. Begin gentle, steady blowing directly into the base of    ║
║        the nest where the coal sits.                             ║
║    6b. Rate: 1 breath per 2 seconds. Each breath 2–3 seconds     ║
║        long. Do NOT blow hard — oxygen, not force, is needed.    ║
║    6c. After 10–20 seconds: smoke increases dramatically.        ║
║    6d. After 15–30 seconds: nest bursts into flame.              ║
║    6e. Hold nest away from face, continue blowing, place into    ║
║        prepared kindling bundle.                                 ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  SKILLS REQUIRED                                                 ║
║    • Strike technique: 20–30 practice strikes to find right      ║
║      angle and speed. Most beginners strike too slowly.          ║
║    • Coal recognition: glowing spot may be tiny (3 mm).          ║
║      Work in shade — easier to see.                              ║
║    • Blowing: steady, not frantic. Panicked blowing kills coal.  ║
║                                                                  ║
║  TRAINING TIME TO 95% RELIABILITY: 2 hours practice             ║
║  CYCLE TIME (trained operator): 15–45 seconds total             ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  FAILURE MODES AND FIXES                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  Problem: No sparks.                                             ║
║    Fix: Check flint edge is fresh (re-knap if rounded). Strike   ║
║    faster. Ensure flint (Mohs 7) is HARDER than pyrite (Mohs 6).║
║                                                                  ║
║  Problem: Sparks but char cloth won't catch.                     ║
║    Fix: Char cloth is damp — warm near any existing heat source  ║
║    for 5 min before retry. OR char cloth not fully charred       ║
║    (brown patches = not done) → re-char [→ R011].               ║
║                                                                  ║
║  Problem: Coal dies before tinder nest lights.                   ║
║    Fix: Blow earlier, more steadily. Tinder too moist → dry      ║
║    tinder more. Nest too tight → loosen the hollow.             ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PRODUCTION NOTES                                                ║
║    • Teach this skill to 10 people per day (each can learn in    ║
║      2 hours). Priority: every team leader must master this.     ║
║    • Each fire kit (pyrite + flint + 10 char cloths) is good     ║
║      for 100+ ignitions.                                         ║
║    • One 200g pyrite nodule lasts ~500 strikes.                  ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
