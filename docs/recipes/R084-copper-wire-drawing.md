# R084 — Copper Wire Drawing

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R084 · COPPER WIRE DRAWING                              ║
║  End product: 100 m copper wire, 1.5 mm diameter               ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHAT YOU GET                                                    ║
║    Fine copper wire for coiling into electromagnets, telegraph  ║
║    lines, dynamo windings. This single capability — making wire  ║
║    — unlocks: telegraph, telephone, electric motors, generators, ║
║    transformers, and all electrical technology.                  ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS                                                     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) 1.5 kg pure copper rod, 8 mm diameter     [→ R030]         ║
║     Annealed (soft). Cold-worked copper breaks in drawing dies.  ║
║     To anneal: heat to 500°C, quench in water (unlike steel,    ║
║     water-quenching SOFTENS copper).                             ║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) Tallow lubricant (for drawing dies)       [→ R051]         ║
║     100g per 100 m of wire drawn.                                ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • Drawing plate (iron, later steel)         [→ R041]         ║
║      A 15×5×1 cm steel bar with a series of tapered holes:      ║
║      Holes: 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2.5, 2.0, 1.5 mm diameter.         ║
║      Each hole: tapered from 15° approach angle to final dia.   ║
║      MAKE THIS FROM TOOL STEEL (R041). Iron holes wear too fast. ║
║                                                                  ║
║    • Drawing bench (wooden)                    [→ R000-I] TERM.║
║      2 m × 20 cm × 15 cm timber. One end: drawing plate clamped.║
║      Other end: wooden windlass (reel), handle, 50 cm arm.      ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 1 — MAKE DRAWING PLATE                                     ║
║    1a. Forge tool steel bar (R041), 15×5×1 cm.                  ║
║    1b. MARK 8 hole positions, equally spaced (15 mm apart).      ║
║    1c. For each hole: drill or punch pilot hole from BOTH sides  ║
║        (tapered approach), file to taper. Use successive:        ║
║        Rough: square file, Round file, Needle file.             ║
║    1d. Final hole diameters (use copper test rod to verify):    ║
║        Position 1: 7.0mm (start here with 8mm rod)             ║
║        Position 2: 6.0mm                                        ║
║        Position 3: 5.0mm                                        ║
║        Position 4: 4.0mm                                        ║
║        Position 5: 3.0mm                                        ║
║        Position 6: 2.5mm                                        ║
║        Position 7: 2.0mm                                        ║
║        Position 8: 1.5mm (target)                               ║
║    1e. Harden plate [→ R041, Phase 5-6]. Temper to blue (300°C).║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 2 — PREPARE COPPER ROD                                     ║
║    2a. Take 1.5 kg copper ingot. Hammer at 800°C to 8 mm rod,   ║
║        4 m long (re-heat as needed, 10+ heats).                  ║
║    2b. Anneal: heat rod to bright red (500–600°C), quench in    ║
║        water bucket. Copper becomes dead soft.                   ║
║    2c. File/grind one end of rod to a taper: 15 cm tapered to   ║
║        6.5 mm to fit through first die (7 mm hole).             ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 3 — DRAW                                                   ║
║    3a. Apply tallow to die face and rod tip.                     ║
║    3b. Push tapered end through first hole (7 mm). Grip with    ║
║        pliers (tongs grip marks wire — use smooth iron tongs).  ║
║    3c. Pull continuously through hole using drawing bench:       ║
║        — Thread wire end through hole, attach to windlass reel  ║
║        — Rotate windlass handle: wire pulled through die        ║
║        — Rate: steady pull, ~0.5 m/sec. Too fast: wire breaks.  ║
║    3d. After 1 m pulled: RE-ANNEAL. Copper work-hardens         ║
║        dramatically in drawing. If not annealed every 1-2 m:   ║
║        wire snaps. Heat to 500°C, water quench. Continue.        ║
║    3e. Advance through each die in sequence: 7→6→5→4→3→2.5→2→1.5mm║
║        Each die reduces diameter ~15%. Don't skip a die.         ║
║    3f. Anneal between EVERY die change.                          ║
║                                                                  ║
║  STEP 4 — WIND AND STORE                                         ║
║    4a. Wind wire on 15 cm dia wooden spool as it draws.         ║
║    4b. Final anneal before use in coils (soft wire bends without ║
║        kinking — vital for clean coil winding).                  ║
║    4c. Store wound on spool, covered from dust.                  ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  YIELD: 1.5 kg copper rod → ~1.4 kg wire = ~100 m at 1.5mm dia.║
║  CYCLE TIME: 4 hours per 100 m (including annealing).           ║
║  INSULATION: Wrap wire with waxed cotton thread [→ R085]:       ║
║    One layer of 0.1 mm cotton thread, helically wound, then      ║
║    beeswax (60°C melt) applied and rubbed in. Cool. Second wrap.║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
