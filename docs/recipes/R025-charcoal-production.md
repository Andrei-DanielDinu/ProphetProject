# R025 — Charcoal Production (Earth Clamp Method)

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  RECIPE R025 · CHARCOAL PRODUCTION — EARTH CLAMP                ║
║  End product: 250 kg charcoal from 1,000 kg dry hardwood        ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  WHAT YOU GET                                                    ║
║    Dense, black carbon with ~85% C content.                      ║
║    Energy density: 30 MJ/kg (vs. dry wood: 18 MJ/kg).           ║
║    Burns hotter than wood and without steam-cooling effect.      ║
║    Essential for all metal smelting — wood alone cannot sustain  ║
║    >700°C in a forced-draft furnace.                             ║
║    Minimum temperature needed: copper smelting 1,083°C;         ║
║    iron smelting 1,200°C. Only charcoal gets there.             ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  INGREDIENTS                                                     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  A) 1,000 kg dry hardwood, pieces 10–20 cm dia  [→ R000-I] TERM║
║     Acacia, tamarisk, or sycamore. MUST be seasoned:            ║
║     — Moisture content <20% (pick up piece: feels light for      ║
║       size; green wood feels heavy and cool).                    ║
║     — Splits cleanly with axe (green wood crushes/splinters).   ║
║     Cut into 60–90 cm lengths for stacking.                     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  B) ~200 kg dry soil / sand (for covering)      [→ R000-F/G]   ║
║     Sandy loam, low clay content. Too much clay → cracks when   ║
║     heated → air leaks → fire → ash instead of charcoal.        ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  TOOLS                                                           ║
║    • Copper axe (for wood cutting)              [→ R030]        ║
║    • Wooden paddle (for soil cover)             [→ R000-I] TERM.║
║    • Bucket of water (for sealing leaks)        [→ R000-H] TERM.║
║    • Long stick (3 m, for chimney hole control) ■ TERMINAL      ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PROCESS                                                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  PHASE 1 — SITE AND STACK PREPARATION (Day 1, 4 hours, 4 workers║
║                                                                  ║
║  1a. Choose site: flat, away from buildings. Minimum 20 m from  ║
║      any thatched structure. Near dry wood source.               ║
║                                                                  ║
║  1b. Clear 4 m diameter circle of vegetation.                    ║
║                                                                  ║
║  1c. Set a central pole: drive a 2 m wooden stake into centre.  ║
║      This becomes the chimney channel.                           ║
║                                                                  ║
║  1d. Stack wood around the pole in a beehive mound:             ║
║      — First ring: lean 90 cm lengths against pole, bottom-out, ║
║        tightly packed.                                           ║
║      — Second ring: lean more wood against first ring. Build     ║
║        up and outward. Final height: 1.5 m. Diameter: 3.5 m.    ║
║      — Fill gaps as you go — no large air spaces in the stack.  ║
║      — Reserve a few small pieces to fill the bottom ring gap   ║
║        (stoking access — sealed after lighting).                 ║
║                                                                  ║
║  1e. TOTAL WOOD: 1,000 kg in one clamp requires 60–80 pieces at ║
║      15 kg each. This is a medium-sized clamp.                   ║
║                                                                  ║
║  PHASE 2 — COVERING (Day 1, 2 hours)                            ║
║                                                                  ║
║  2a. Cover entire mound with 10 cm of dry grass, straw, or      ║
║      leaves. This thermal layer prevents the soil from cooling  ║
║      the outer wood too fast (reduces charcoal from outside in). ║
║                                                                  ║
║  2b. Shovel dry sandy soil over the grass layer. 15 cm thick    ║
║      minimum. Compact gently with back of paddle.                ║
║                                                                  ║
║  2c. Leave three things exposed:                                 ║
║      — Top of central pole (chimney hole — 10 cm dia)           ║
║      — 3 × air inlet holes at base, equally spaced, 10 cm dia   ║
║        (made by pushing a stick in and removing it).             ║
║      — One larger access at bottom: 20 cm hole for lighting.    ║
║                                                                  ║
║  PHASE 3 — LIGHTING (Day 1, 30 min)                             ║
║                                                                  ║
║  3a. Light small tinder fire at base access hole. Push glowing  ║
║      embers into centre.                                         ║
║  3b. Blow gently. When internal fire catches (smoke rises from  ║
║      chimney hole): seal base access hole with wet soil.         ║
║  3c. Watch chimney hole: smoke rises. For first 2 hours: white  ║
║      smoke (steam from wood). After that: blue-grey smoke        ║
║      (pyrolysis gases). Dense white smoke always = moisture.     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  PHASE 4 — MANAGING THE BURN (Day 2–3, constant attention)      ║
║                                                                  ║
║  4a. CRITICAL: The mound must never get too much air (burns to  ║
║      ash) or too little (goes out before full carbonisation).    ║
║                                                                  ║
║  4b. Air management via inlet holes:                             ║
║      — Too much smoke, little heat (too little air): open 1–2   ║
║        base inlets wider.                                        ║
║      — Mound is sinking rapidly (burning too fast, too much air):║
║        partially block inlets with wet soil.                     ║
║                                                                  ║
║  4c. Watch for cracks in soil cover: seal with wet soil         ║
║      immediately. Cracks = air enters = fire zone = ash.         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  4d. Mound subsides as wood carbonises: press top down gently   ║
║      and add more soil cover over any exposed gaps.              ║
║                                                                  ║
║  4e. After 48–72 hours: chimney smoke turns from blue-grey to   ║
║      almost transparent. This signals completion.                ║
║                                                                  ║
║  PHASE 5 — SEALING AND COOLING (Day 3–4)                        ║
║                                                                  ║
║  5a. When smoke is nearly clear: seal ALL openings with wet soil.║
║      Including chimney hole: press wet soil into top.            ║
║                                                                  ║
║  5b. Leave sealed for minimum 24 hours. Charcoal inside is very ║
║      hot (400–600°C) and WILL reignite if exposed to air now.   ║
║                                                                  ║
║  5c. After 24–48 hours: open from bottom. Shovel out charcoal.  ║
║      Good charcoal: uniform black, rings metallically when       ║
║      pieces knocked together, leaves dark streak on stone (not   ║
║      grey ash streak), no brown patches.                         ║
║                                                                  ║
║  5d. REJECT any pieces that are:                                 ║
║      — Grey/ashy: burned to ash — discard                        ║
║      — Brown: under-carbonised — re-process in a small batch    ║
║      — Good (uniform black): store in dry covered area           ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  YIELD AND PERFORMANCE                                           ║
║    INPUT: 1,000 kg dry hardwood.                                 ║
║    OUTPUT: 200–280 kg charcoal (20–28% yield by weight).        ║
║    Rule of thumb: 4 kg dry wood → 1 kg charcoal.                ║
║                                                                  ║
║    QUALITY CHECK:                                                ║
║    • Density: good charcoal ≈ 400–500 kg/m³.                     ║
║    • Burns: in forced draft, reaches 1,100°C (open flame zone). ║
║    • 1 kg charcoal: burns ~20 min at peak temperature in bellows-║
║      assisted fire.                                              ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  SKILLS                                                          ║
║    • Stacking: 1 day practice to master pattern.                 ║
║    • Burn management: 3 clamp runs to master.                    ║
║    • Key skill: reading smoke colour and responding correctly.   ║
║    TRAINING TIME: 1 week to reliable production.                 ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  PRODUCTION SCALING                                              ║
║    • 1 clamp (1,000 kg wood → 250 kg charcoal): 3 days burn +   ║
║      2 days cool = 5 days per batch.                             ║
║    • To run 2 kilns + 1 bloomery continuously:                   ║
║      Need: 100 kg charcoal/day minimum.                         ║
║      Requires: 1 clamp every 2.5 days = 2 running in rotation.  ║
║    • Assign: 2 charcoal workers full-time (cutting, stacking,    ║
║      managing), plus 2 woodcutters to supply 1 tonne dry wood   ║
║      per clamp.                                                  ║
║    • Year 1 target: 1 tonne charcoal stockpile before first     ║
║      smelting run begins.                                        ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```
