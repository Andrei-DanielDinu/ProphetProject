# Phase 4: Industrial Chemistry, Steam, and Precision Manufacturing (Years 1200–2200)

> "Industrialization begins when machines can build more accurate machines, when thermal energy replaces muscle, and when heavy chemical reagents can be manufactured continuously."

---

## 1. Phase Charter

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Elapsed Time** | Years 1200 to 2200 |
| **Primary Objective** | Build self-reproducing precision machine tools (slide-rest lathes, boring mills, surface plates); invent condensing steam engines and safety boilers; manufacture mineral coal-coke blast furnaces; mass-produce sulfuric acid and soda ash. |
| **Primary Constraints** | Cylinder boring tolerances (steam blow-by hazards); boiler explosions under steam pressure; toxic chemical exposure from sulfur and lead fumes; coal mine flooding. |
| **Exit Condition** | Wilkinson-style boring mills and slide-rest lathes manufacturing interchangeable machine components; Watt-style condensing steam engines powering factories and transport; lead-chamber sulfuric acid and Solvay soda ash produced in continuous multi-ton chemical plants; Bessemer and open-hearth mass steel production. |

---

## 2. Priority Workstreams

### A. The Precision Machine Tool Bootstrap
- **Whitworth Three-Plate Scraping**: Generate master optical surface plates true to within $2\ \mu\text{m}$ across 1 meter by mutually scraping three cast iron plates against each other with blue marking paste.
- **Maudslay Lead Screw and Slide-Rest Lathe**: Mount rigid tool-holders on slide-rests to eliminate hand tremor; cut master lead screws by splitting nuts and averaging errors; generate standardized, interchangeable screw threads.
- **Cylinder Boring Mills**: Construct rigid double-supported boring bars to bore large steam cylinders true to within $0.8\text{ mm}$ over multi-meter strokes.

### B. Thermal Steam Power and Boilers
- **Atmospheric to Condensing Steam Engines**: Transition from Newcomen atmospheric pumping engines to James Watt's separate-condenser, double-acting rotary steam engines fitted with centrifugal flyball governors.
- **Boiler Safety Engineering**: Fabricate riveted wrought-iron and mild-steel cylindrical boilers; install dual spring/deadweight safety valves; enforce periodic hydrostatic proof testing at $2\times$ working pressure to eliminate boiler explosions.

### C. Coke Metallurgy and Bulk Steelmaking
- **Darby Coke Smelting**: Replace scarce charcoal with carbonized coal (metallurgical coke), building blast furnaces capable of smelting hundreds of tons of liquid pig iron per week.
- **Bessemer Converters**: Blow compressed air through molten iron to burn off carbon exothermically, converting pig iron into structural mild steel ingots in 15 minutes without external fuel.

### D. Heavy Chemical Manufacturing
- **Lead-Chamber Sulfuric Acid**: Burn sulfur and oxidize $\text{SO}_2$ with steam and nitric oxides in giant lead chambers, producing 70% industrial sulfuric acid for metal pickling and fertilizer processing.
- **Solvay Soda Ash**: React ammoniated brine with carbon dioxide from limestone kilns to produce high-purity sodium carbonate without the toxic waste of the Leblanc process.

---

## 3. Exit Criteria Evaluation

1. **Self-Replicating Machine Shops**: Foundries and machine shops can independently replicate every component of a steam engine and machine tool without hand fitting.
2. **Thermal Work Capacity**: Steam engines provide steady, location-independent shaft power exceeding thousands of horsepower.
3. **Chemical Scale**: Sulfuric acid and soda ash are available in multi-ton quantities for textiles, glass, metallurgy, and early chemical fertilizers.
