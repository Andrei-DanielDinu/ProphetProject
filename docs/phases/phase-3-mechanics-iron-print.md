# Phase 3: Mechanical Power, Iron, Optics, and Reproducible Knowledge (Years 500–1200)

> "The breakthrough is not an individual mechanism; it is the ability to harness non-muscular energy, cut hardened iron with quench-tempered steel, observe the microscopic world through polished glass, and duplicate technical books at the speed of a printing press."

---

## 1. Phase Charter

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Elapsed Time** | Years 500 to 1200 |
| **Primary Objective** | Deploy water and wind kinetic energy to industrial milling and forge hammers; establish systematic bloomery iron and tempered steel production; invent movable-type printing; manufacture optical lenses for compound microscopes and astronomical telescopes. |
| **Primary Constraints** | Refractory furnace limits for iron smelting; timber depletion for charcoal fuel; glass purity and grinding tolerances for distortion-free lenses; mechanical gear wear and backlash. |
| **Exit Condition** | Waterwheels and windmills performing staple grinding and forge work across all major river basins; bloomery iron replacing bronze as the everyday tool metal; movable-type printing presses operating in public libraries; scientific instrument makers producing calibrated microscopes and clocks. |

---

## 2. Priority Workstreams

### A. Non-Muscular Mechanical Power
- **Waterwheel Networks**: Construct overshot, breastshot, and undershot waterwheels with heavy timber framing and iron gudgeon axle pins.
- **Drive Train Kinematics**: Fabricate wooden right-angle crown and lantern pinions, cams for trip-hammers, and crank-connecting rod linkages converting rotary shaft motion into reciprocating saw and pump strokes.
- **Tide and Wind Power**: In coastal and desert delta areas lacking high hydrostatic head, construct post-mills and horizontal-axis windmills for grain grinding and saline water drainage.

### B. Bloomery Iron and Quench-Tempered Steel
- **Iron Shaft Furnaces**: Build clay-limestone bloomery furnaces operating at $1200^\circ\text{C}$ with continuous forced-air bellows. Smelt hematite ores using carbon monoxide reduction, separating liquid fayalite slag from solid sponge iron.
- **Water-Powered Forge Hammers**: Use cam-driven water trip-hammers (100–300 kg iron heads) to hammer consolidated iron blooms, expelling slag inclusions and rolling wrought iron bars.
- **Carburization and Tempering**: Heat wrought iron in charcoal packs to create high-carbon tool steel ($0.8\%\text{ C}$). Quench in water from cherry red ($800^\circ\text{C}$) to form hard martensite, then temper at $250^\circ\text{C}$ to produce tough files, cold chisels, and cutting plane irons.

### C. Precision Optics and Scientific Observation
- **Optical Glass Melting**: Formulate clear, bubble-free crown glass using washed white silica sand and purified potash flux. Anneal blocks over weeks to relieve internal thermal stresses.
- **Spherical Lens Grinding**: Grind glass blanks against cast-iron laps using progressively finer emery abrasive slurry; polish on pitch laps with tin oxide powder.
- **Compound Microscopes**: Assemble compound microscopes with objective and ocular lenses achieving $50\times\text{ to } 300\times$ optical magnification, unlocking direct empirical study of cellular anatomy, blood capillaries, and bacteria.

### D. Rags-to-Paper and Movable Type Printing
- **Paper Pulping Mills**: Convert discarded linen and cotton rags into cellulose pulp using water-driven stampers; press sheets onto wire-mesh molds and size with animal gelatin.
- **Movable Metal Type**: Cut master steel punches; strike copper matrices; cast reusable alloy type (80% lead, 15% antimony, 5% tin) that expands on cooling to fill sharp letter corners.
- **Screw Presses**: Build wooden screw printing presses turning out 250 sheets per hour, distributing verified engineering drawings, agricultural guides, and medical manuals across public regional libraries.

---

## 3. Exit Criteria Evaluation

1. **Energy Independence**: More than $60\%$ of heavy mechanical milling, drainage, and forge hammering is driven by water or wind rather than human or animal muscle.
2. **Material Abundance**: Iron and steel tools are produced locally at costs an order of magnitude lower than bronze.
3. **Knowledge Redundancy**: More than 10,000 copies of standard technical, medical, and scientific encyclopedias are distributed across distributed libraries, rendering loss of knowledge impossible.
