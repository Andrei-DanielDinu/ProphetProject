# Operational Manual 02: Geological Prospecting and Mining Atlas

> "Before you can smelt or synthesize, you must find the veins in the earth. This atlas gives the exact physical locations, identification tests, and extraction quotas for the foundational minerals of Egypt and the Sinai."

---

## 1. Field Identification Tests for the Prospector

When surveying desert wadis and mountain screes, identify minerals using four deterministic field tests:

1. **Streak Test**: Rub the mineral across the unglazed white bottom rim of an earthenware ceramic bowl. The color of the pulverized powder is an immutable diagnostic fingerprint.
2. **Mohs Hardness Scratch Test**:
   - Fingernail scratches minerals up to hardness $2.5$ (Gypsum, Talc).
   - A copper chisel scratches up to $3.5$ (Calcite, soft Malachite).
   - An iron/bronze blade scratches up to $5.5$ (Apatite, Galena).
   - Hardened flint/quartz scratches up to $7.0$ (Feldspar, Magnetite).
3. **Acid Effervescence Test**: Drop dilute vinegar (acetic acid) or fermented sour wine onto the mineral. Vigorous bubbling ($CO_2$ release) confirms carbonate ores (Malachite, Limestone, Natron).
4. **Specific Gravity (Heft Test)**: Metallic ores feel unnaturally heavy in the palm compared to common limestone or quartz granite of identical size.

## 3. Targeted Deposits, Locations, and Gathering Quotas

### A. Copper Ores: Malachite & Chrysocolla
- **Exact Coordinates**:
  - **Wadi Dara**: Eastern Desert foothills, $27.9^\circ\text{N}, 33.2^\circ\text{E}$.
  - **Serabit el-Khadim / Wadi Maghara**: South-central Sinai, $29.0^\circ\text{N}, 33.4^\circ\text{E}$.
  - **Timna Basin**: Northern Arabah valley, $29.8^\circ\text{N}, 34.9^\circ\text{E}$.
- **Geological Occurrence**: Hydrothermal veins cutting Precambrian granite basement rocks and overlying Cambrian sandstone margins.
- **Visual Appearance**: Vivid emerald-green crusts (malachite) and vibrant turquoise-blue stains (chrysocolla) coating weathered white quartz veins.
- **Diagnostic Tests**: Bright pale green streak; effervesces in acid; hardness $3.5\text{--}4.0$; specific gravity $4.0$.
- **Extraction Quota**: **1,000 kg hand-sorted ore** per smelting run. Cobbing (hammering off waste host rock) on-site leaves $\approx 400\text{ kg}$ concentrated ore containing $25\text{--}35\%$ copper, yielding **$100\text{ to } 120\text{ kg}$ pure metallic copper**.

---

### B. Lead and Silver: Galena ($\text{PbS}$)
- **Exact Coordinates**: **Gebel el-Rosas** ("Mountain of Lead"), Eastern Desert coastal range, $25.4^\circ\text{N}, 34.6^\circ\text{E}$.
- **Geological Occurrence**: Replacement veins and pocket lenses inside coastal Miocene coral reef limestones.
- **Visual Appearance**: Brilliant metallic lead-grey cubic crystals that fracture cleanly along three perpendicular planes.
- **Diagnostic Tests**: Heavy in the hand (specific gravity $7.6$); lead-grey metallic streak; melts at low campfire temperatures ($327^\circ\text{C}$); leaves a sulfurous smell when heated.
- **Extraction Quota**: **500 kg raw galena**. Easily hand-cobbed to $> 70\%$ lead purity. Smelts to **$300\text{ kg}$ lead metal** for pipe liners, acid chamber plates, and type metal, with cupellation yielding $\approx 150\text{ grams}$ fine silver.

---

### C. Iron Ores: Oolitic Hematite ($\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$) & Magnetite
- **Exact Coordinates**:
  - **Aswan Sedimentary Beds**: East bank of the Nile directly north of Aswan, $24.1^\circ\text{N}, 32.9^\circ\text{E}$.
  - **Bahariya Oasis**: Western Desert depression, $28.3^\circ\text{N}, 28.9^\circ\text{E}$.
- **Geological Occurrence**: Thick, horizontal sedimentary beds composed of small round mineral beads (oolites) cemented with iron silicate.
- **Visual Appearance**: Dense, heavy, dark red to purplish-black rock layers exposed on plateau surfaces.
- **Diagnostic Tests**: **Blood-red to cherry-red streak**; hardness $5.5\text{--}6.5$; specific gravity $5.2$. Does not effervesce in cold acid.
- **Extraction Quota**: **2,000 kg oolitic hematite**. Crushed to walnut-sized nodules for the bloomery furnace, yielding **$350\text{ to } 500\text{ kg}$ consolidated wrought iron**.

---

### D. Alkaline Salts: Natron ($\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 \cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{NaHCO}_3$)
- **Exact Coordinates**: **Wadi Natrun**, desert depression 80 km northwest of Memphis, $30.4^\circ\text{N}, 30.3^\circ\text{E}$.
- **Geological Occurrence**: Seasonal hyper-saline alkaline lakes fed by underground seepage through sodium-rich sands. During summer heat, lakes evaporate down to thick crystalline crusts.
- **Visual Appearance**: White, snow-like crystalline salt crusts 5 to 20 cm thick along lake shores.
- **Diagnostic Tests**: Bitter, alkaline soapy taste; dissolves readily in water to yield a slippery, basic solution ($\text{pH } 10\text{--}11$); fizzes violently in acid.
- **Extraction Quota**: **5,000 kg raw salt**. Scraped into palm-leaf baskets. Essential as the chemical feedstock for soda ash glass, quicklime saponification for soap, textile scouring, and surgical drying.

---

### E. Elemental Sulfur ($\text{S}$)
- **Exact Coordinates**: **Gemsa Peninsula**, Red Sea coast at the entrance to the Gulf of Suez, $27.7^\circ\text{N}, 33.6^\circ\text{E}$.
- **Geological Occurrence**: Native elemental sulfur filling pockets within gypsum and evaporite limestone beds.
- **Visual Appearance**: Bright canary-yellow crystalline nodules and powdered crusts.
- **Diagnostic Tests**: Distinctive yellow streak; low melting point ($115^\circ\text{C}$); burns with a distinct blue flame emitting pungent, choking sulfur dioxide ($\text{SO}_2$) gas.
- **Extraction Quota**: **500 kg native sulfur**. Purified by melting and straining through coarse linen. Critical for sulfuric acid synthesis and pyrotechnic ignition.

---

### F. Petroleum Bitumen & Crude Oil Seeps
- **Exact Coordinates**: **Gebel el-Zeit** ("Mountain of Oil"), Red Sea coast, $27.9^\circ\text{N}, 33.5^\circ\text{E}$.
- **Geological Occurrence**: Surface oil seeps escaping along major active tectonic faults bordering the Gulf of Suez rift basin.
- **Visual Appearance**: Thick, viscous, pitch-black liquid petroleum bubbling out of fractures and pooling in desert depressions, hardening to glassy black asphalt.
- **Diagnostic Tests**: Burns with a dense black smoky flame; insoluble in water; dissolves in alcohol; pungent hydrocarbon tar smell.
- **Extraction Quota**: **20 large ceramic storage amphorae ($\approx 600\text{ liters}$)**. Used for waterproofing ship hulls, making tough composite hafting mastics, and fuel for high-temperature closed lamps.
