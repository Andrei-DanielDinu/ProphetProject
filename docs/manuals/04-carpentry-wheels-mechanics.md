# Operational Manual 04: Carpentry, The Wheel, and Axle Mechanics

> "Do not attempt to make a wheel by slicing a round disc from a tree trunk; it will split along the grain and shatter on the first rock. A wheel is an engineered composite structure of joined wood, bronze bearings, and compressive tire hoops."

---

## 1. Timber Selection and Seasoning

Egypt lacks vast forests, but possesses native hardwoods capable of extreme mechanical endurance if selected correctly:

| Wood Species | Egyptian Name | Physical Characteristics | Ideal Application |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acacia nilotica** | *Sunt* | Extremely dense, interlocked grain, highly resistant to termites and water rot. | Wheel hubs, teeth of gears, axles, dowels, mallets. |
| **Tamarisk** | *Asr* | Tough, fibrous, cross-grained, flexible. | Wheel felloes (rims), tool handles, boat ribs. |
| **Sycamore Fig** | *Nehet* | Medium density, easy to split and shape, uniform grain. | Wagon planks, siding, dowel pins. |
| **Cedar of Lebanon** | *Ash* | Imported via Byblos; long, perfectly straight grain, aromatic resinous rot resistance. | Master axle beams, long ship keels, structural rafters. |

### The Seasoning Protocol (Preventing Warping and Radial Checking)
1. **Felling**: Cut trees during midwinter when sap flow is minimal.
2. **Bark Stripping**: Strip the outer green bark immediately with an adze to eliminate wood-boring beetle larvae.
3. **End-Grain Sealing**: Moisture evaporates from the end grain 10 times faster than from the radial face, causing catastrophic radial splitting (checking). Coat the cut log ends immediately with melted animal tallow, bitumen, or thick mud.
4. **Stacking**: Stack rough-hewn planks off the ground on level stone piers with 3-cm dry wooden spacers (stickers) placed between every layer. Keep under a shaded, open-sided drying shed with continuous wind circulation.
5. **Drying Duration**: Season for **90 days per 2.5 cm of plank thickness**. Moisture content drops from $> 50\%$ down to $< 12\%$, stopping dimensional warping.

## 2. Blueprint: The Tripartite Solid Wooden Wheel

A single tree trunk slice ("cookie") splits radially along medullary rays under load. The earliest durable heavy-load wheel is the **Tripartite Wheel**, assembled from three separate planks joined with mortise-and-tenon joints:

```
                  +--------------------------------+
                  |     Curved Outer Segment A     |
                  +=========+============+=========+  <--- Through Mortise & Tenon Joints
                  |         |  [ Hub ]   |         |
                  |         |  (Bore)    |         |  <--- Central Main Plank B (Spindle Bore)
                  |         |            |         |
                  +=========+============+=========+  <--- Through Mortise & Tenon Joints
                  |     Curved Outer Segment C     |
                  +--------------------------------+
                                   |
                  <--- Rawhide or Bronze Tire Rim --->
```

### Step-by-Step Construction Procedure

#### Step 1: Joinery of the Three Blanks
1. Hew three seasoned acacia planks, each $10\text{ cm}$ thick, $35\text{ cm}$ wide, and $100\text{ cm}$ long.
2. Plane the mating longitudinal edges perfectly flat using a bronze adze and sandstone rubbing block.
3. Chisel three matching rectangular mortise pockets ($5\text{ cm}$ deep, $8\text{ cm}$ wide, $2\text{ cm}$ thick) into the adjoining edges of the planks.
4. Carve matching dry hardwood tenons (loose tongues). Coat tenons with hot hide glue or pine-resin mastic, press the three planks together, and drill $2\text{-cm}$ holes through the plank face directly through each tenon.
5. Drive tapered dry hardwood dowel pegs through the holes. The dowels lock the three planks into a single, rigid monolithic wooden slab.

#### Step 2: Scribing and Cutting the Circle
1. Locate the exact geometrical center of the central plank.
2. Drive a small bronze pin into the center; loop a non-stretching linen cord measuring $45\text{ cm}$ to a bronze awl.
3. Scribe a continuous, perfect circle ($90\text{ cm}$ outer diameter) across the face of all three joined planks.
4. Cut away the waste corners using a bronze pull-saw, finishing the circular curve with a curved adze and coarse flint rasp.

#### Step 3: Boring the Hub (The Nave)
1. At the exact scribed center, bore a circular pilot hole using a bow drill fitted with a hollow copper tube and sharp quartz sand abrasive slurry.
2. Enlarge the hole with a curved gouge chisel to a finished diameter of **$10\text{ cm}$**.
3. Carve two circular wooden hub strengthening plates ($25\text{ cm}$ diameter, $6\text{ cm}$ thick with matching $10\text{-cm}$ center holes). Bolt them with hardwood dowels to both faces of the wheel. This triples the bearing support length from $10\text{ cm}$ to $22\text{ cm}$, preventing the wheel from wobbling laterally on its axle.

## 3. The Axle, Bearing, and Linchpin Assembly

```
  [ Wagon Chassis Frame ]
             |
  +----------v---------------------------------------------------+
  | Solid Hardwood Axle Beam (Acacia)                            |
  +-----------------------------------+--------------------------+
                                      |
                                      v (Turned Axle Spindle: 9.8 cm dia)
                           +----------+----------+
                           |  Inner Bronze Ring  |
                           |  [ Wheel Hub ]      |  <--- Pressed-fit Bronze Bushing
                           |  Outer Bronze Ring  |
                           +----------+----------+
                                      |
                     [ Linchpin Hole ]+==[ Bronze Linchpin Peg ]
```

### Stationary Axle vs. Rotating Axle
- Do not fix the wheels rigidly to a rotating axle; when a wagon turns, the outer wheel must travel along a larger radius arc than the inner wheel. A rigid axle forces one wheel to drag and skid, snapping wooden axles under load.
- **The Design**: The main horizontal axle beam is **bolted rigidly to the underside of the wagon chassis**. The wheel hubs rotate freely and independently around the smoothed axle spindles on either side.

### The Low-Friction Bronze Sleeve Bushing
1. Cast two split cylindrical bronze half-sleeves ($88\%\text{ Cu}, 12\%\text{ Sn}$, $4\text{ mm}$ wall thickness).
2. Heat the bronze sleeves and press them into the wooden bore of the wheel hub. The bronze provides a hard, glass-smooth bearing surface that will not wear oval like raw wood.
3. Turn the ends of the acacia axle beam down to an exact diameter of $9.8\text{ cm}$ (providing a $1.0\text{ mm}$ running clearance inside the $10.0\text{-cm}$ bronze hub bore).

### Lubrication Formulation
Raw animal fat rots and oxidizes quickly. Formulate **Industrial Wheel Tallow**:
- Render beef or sheep kidney suet in a ceramic pot over low heat; strain through linen to remove cell tissue.
- Mix $85\%$ liquid suet with $10\%$ beeswax (raises melting point so the lubricant does not liquefy in desert sun) and $5\%$ fine powdered wood charcoal or graphite (solid lubricant providing dry boundary lubrication if oil film breaks).
- Coat the axle spindle liberally before mounting the wheel.

### The Linchpin Retainer
- Slide a flat bronze washer over the spindle against the outer hub face.
- Drill a vertical $2\text{-cm}$ hole through the protruding end of the wooden axle.
- Insert a heavy forged bronze or oak pin (the **linchpin**), securing the lower split end with a rawhide loop. The linchpin prevents the wheel from sliding off during travel across uneven wadi tracks.

## 4. The Shrink-Fit Tire (Hoop Compression)

A wooden wheel rolling over granite rocks will splinter around its perimeter unless protected by a compressive hoop:

### The Rawhide Method (Immediate Field Capability)
1. Cut strips of fresh, un-tanned cattle rawhide ($10\text{ cm}$ wide, $5\text{ mm}$ thick).
2. Soak in water for 4 hours until completely saturated and elastic.
3. Stretch the wet rawhide tightly around the circumference of the wooden wheel rim, overlapping the ends by $15\text{ cm}$, and nail in place with short bronze studs.
4. Place the wheel in the hot desert sun. As the rawhide dehydrates, **it shrinks violently by $12\text{--}15\%$**, clamping the three planks together with tremendous inward compressive pre-stress. The hardened dried rawhide wears like hard plastic and protects the wood from rock impact.

### The Thermal Bronze/Iron Tire Upgrade (Phase 2+)
- Forge a flat metal hoop with an inside diameter exactly **$5\text{ mm}$ smaller** than the wooden wheel's outside diameter.
- Build a circular wood fire and heat the metal hoop evenly until it glows dull red. Thermal expansion ($\Delta L = \alpha L \Delta T$) expands the hoop diameter by $\approx 10\text{ mm}$.
- Lift the glowing hoop with blacksmith tongs, drop it over the wooden wheel, and immediately douse with buckets of cold Nile water.
- The metal contracts instantly, locking the wooden wheel in permanent compressive hoop stress without requiring nails or glue.
